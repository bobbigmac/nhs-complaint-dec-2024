Author: WAYNE LARGE
Date: a year ago
Rating: 1 star
Possibly one of the most disappointing experiences I’ve ever had with senior doctors. After being told by district nurses and undertakers that I could collect a death certificate to register my parent’s passing, I arrived at the practice only to be informed that the procedure had changed. Neither the nurses nor the detailed NHS leaflet I was given reflected this change, leading to unnecessary confusion at an already difficult time. When I asked to speak to the manager, I was instead met by her secretary, who I initially assumed was the manager until informed otherwise. Despite requesting the manager again and asking for the new procedure in writing, I was then redirected to a junior doctor. They asked me to fill out a form I had already completed with the district nurse, which then had to be emailed. To make matters worse, I later received a call from a senior doctor accusing me of upsetting and being rude to staff—a claim that was both unfounded and deeply upsetting. This same doctor, during a previous home visit, did not even know my parent’s name. On the phone, her tone was condescending and defensive when I questioned her. Over recent months, during an incredibly emotional period, I have consistently found this practice to be unhelpful, lacking in empathy, and frequently contradictory in their communication. Sensitive matters were often discussed in the crowded reception area, which felt completely inappropriate and disrespectful. This experience added unnecessary stress during bereavement, and I sincerely hope the practice takes steps to improve their procedures and communication in the future.
