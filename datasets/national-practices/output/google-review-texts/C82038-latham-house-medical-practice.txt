For routine appointments, we ask patients to complete a short online form. Once submitted, your GP reviews each request within two working days and provides advice, next steps, or arranges care as appropriate. If your GP feels a face-to-face or telephone appointment is clinically necessary, they can offer one within one to four weeks, or the earliest routine appointment will be offered. Practice response date: 3 months ago
Practice response:
Dear Lilly, We are sincerely sorry to read your review of your experience. At the Practice, we offer an on the day clinic called Immediate access (IAC) but it's important to distinguish that this is not emergency care. The on the day clinic can only offer primary care support, as we do in routine appointments, and this is very different to the capabilities offered at A&E or at a walk in care in a hospital setting (secondary care). In addition, we have limited daily capacity in this clinic and when we reach it, we must inform NHS 111 and advise patients to seek same day care in another setting locally. This is a standard NHS process. We are pleased to understand from your review that you were supported appropriately in hospital on the same date. In answer to your additional concerns: Prescription requests need to be submitted by the patient, and all prescriptions are signed by a GP for authorisation. We work to have urgent or on the day prescriptions requests signed by the end of the working day by the duty GP. Routine or repeat prescriptions can be requested online and will be sent to the patient's nominated pharmacy within two working days (or sooner), but please be aware your pharmacy may need a few more days to collate your prescription for you. Doctors’ notes or FIT notes can be requested online from our website. Please get in touch online via our website or the NHS app to request a GP appointment as you need it. We hope this response helps to support you.

Practice response date: 4 months ago
Practice response:
Dear Richard, we are sorry that our on the day service was at capacity when you contacted us. This is a service we provide for concerns that cannot wait for a routine GP appointment but this is not an emergency service. At this time of year, this clinic tends to address seasonal infections and capacity is reached very quickly. As we have a limited number of appointments in this clinic, when we reach capacity, we notify NHS 111 and we advise patients with urgent needs to visit a walk in service. A walk in service is a hospital site and hospitals have more capacity, more expertise and more equipment vs a GP Practice. A GP Practice will have limitations with on the day support which is also why a Practice clinician may also advise a patient to attend a walk in service, after assessment, if they feel same day investigations are required. If you would like us to investigate your specific experience in more detail, please contact our complaints team and we would be happy to look into this in more detail for you and provide you with a response.
