Author: Jay Renn
Date: 4 years ago
Rating: 2 stars
While doctor surgeries across NI have understandably introduced telephone triage in response to the Covid19 pandemic, telephone triage had already been established at Tandragee Medical Practice on a permanent basis - probably a reflection of the fact that this is a single practitioner surgery (supported by a part-time locum and nursing staff). Telephone triage calls may only be requested from reception during two specific times each day, either 8.00 a.m. (relating solely to that morning's triage calls) or 2.00 p.m. (relating solely to that afternoon's triage calls). Lines are continually busy during the two aforementioned times and at the point of finally getting through to reception, you may find reception advising you that all telephone triage appointments have been allocated. If this happens, your request for a triage call will not be passed on to the GP to deal with during a subsequent session. Instead, you will be asked to try again by calling back during the next 8am/2pm timeslot. This system no doubt operates very well for the surgery staff in terms of minimising distraction from patients, allowing them to neatly compartmentalise and process their workload efficiently. However, this may come at the expense of flexibility and accessibility, from a patient perspective. The system requires patience and persistence to navigate, both of which may be in short supply when you are feeling particularly unwell and/or in pain but when you fall short of being a medical emergency justifying dialling 999. The wisdom of a permanent telephone triage system also relies upon a 100% crossover between those suffering a medical emergency and those who would describe something as a medical emergency. Just some things to be aware of before making a decision to register. Those living in the near area are fortunate enough to enjoy access to other options.
