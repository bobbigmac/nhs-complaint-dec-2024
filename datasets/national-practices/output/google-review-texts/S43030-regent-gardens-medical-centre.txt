I had a similar experience to lexy. I absolutely loved this gp, reception are lovely, nurses and doctors are incredible and caring, had no issue and felt very lucky to be with them. That is until I got a notice saying, cause I missed 3 appointments over multiple years, I’d been removed by upper management and ‘partners’. I gave them the benefit of the doubt and hoped that after they heard about my circumstances (struggle with mental health, having 6 doctors apointment a month per average due to getting multiple diognosisis’ some of which cause struggle with making calls and appointment and short term memory struggles famously and getting this news just as I was getting a potential cancerous lump in my chest checked when I had a history of cancer in my family). Me and my family fought long and hard cause I did truly love this gp but they wouldn’t hear it. Even after a pleading and emotional letter being sent begging them to have any sympathy, they didn’t even have the dignity to contact us back themselves and hid behind a receptionist who couldn’t provide us with anything other than I was still rejected. . I know for a fact if any one my doctors or even the reception had a say they would have empathy and understanding. But the people making this decisions were ‘partners’ that the managerial staff wouldn’t elaborate on and had zero sympathy. I’m lucky that I had the confidence to seek help after many mental health scares but what about those who struggle more? What would something like this happening to them do to them when they fought so hard to get the confidence to seek help? Again, incredible doctors and lovely lovely staff all round, but this policy and the decision being up to people who have no idea who you are or any informantion about you is increibly harmful and scary.
