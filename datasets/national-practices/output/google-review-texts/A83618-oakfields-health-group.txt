Author: The Truth

Rating: 1 star
Equality Act 2010 – failure to make reasonable adjustments / discriminatory approach to a housebound patient. I’m a disabled patient with a long-term, mental-health-related medical condition which leaves me housebound. Oakfields Health Group have handled my request for accessibility in a way I consider discriminatory and a failure to comply with the Equality Act 2010 duty to make reasonable adjustments. 1. Inappropriate gatekeeping by reception: When I initially raised the need for home arrangements, a receptionist told me I was “not truly housebound”. That is not a clinical judgement for reception staff to make. It was distressing, dismissive, and shaped what happened next. 2. Refusal of an adjustment that actually removes the barrier: I requested home blood tests because travel/attendance is not realistically achievable for me due to my housebound disability, and previois GPs have made this adjustment for me no problem. Blood tests are not a luxury — they are a basic, essential part of healthcare access. The practice refused the home adjustment and instead offered alternative arrangements that do not remove the core barrier for a housebound patient. 3. Delays, shut-down tone, and being pushed away: After the practice invited further evidence, I was left waiting a prolonged period for updates. The complaint process dragged on with no reply for months meaning the Practice Manager had to be chased. Then the Practice Manager closed the complaint at practice level and refused to discuss it further, pointing me towards escalation routes. I was also told not to use her direct email address (despite ongoing correspondence) and to use generic channels instead — which, in context, felt like being shut down rather than supported. To date I have still been left without a workable route to access the tests I need. 4. Broken assurances from a previous complaint: This is not the first time. Following a previous complaint last year, Oakfields promised to take reasonable adjustments seriously and to make communication accessible (basic email accessibility only happened after I complained). Now, when it matters most — access to essential investigations — the same pattern has repeated. "Register elsewhere" implication: The Practice Manager suggested I register with a different GP - which reads as "we won't adapt; go away," rather than meeting accessibility duties under the Equality Act. This has left me feeling like a “problem” to be managed rather than a patient to be cared for. A GP practice should be the gateway to services. If a GP practice won’t implement reasonable adjustments for a housebound disabled patient, that is profoundly concerning and sets a terrible example. I’m posting this because I do not want other disabled patients to go through this. If you have a disability, especially a mental-health-related condition that affects travel/access, think very carefully before registering here.
Practice response date: a month ago
Practice response:
