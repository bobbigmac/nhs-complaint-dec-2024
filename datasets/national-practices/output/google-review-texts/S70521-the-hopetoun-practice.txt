Author: Alice Wang Leplar

Rating: 1 star
Earlier this week, I called a few days in advance to try to book a doctor’s appointment for Thursday. During the phone call, I was told that the surgery would not be open on Thursday and that I should go to a pharmacy instead to buy medication. Today is Thursday. I live nearby and happened to walk past the surgery, only to find that it was actually open. I went inside to ask, and the staff confirmed that the surgery is open on Thursdays. I then asked why I had been told on the phone that it was closed. As a result of this incorrect information, I missed the opportunity to make an appointment. I have only recently moved here, and this experience has been quite disappointing. On a previous occasion when I was unwell, by the time my registration was completed and I was able to seek medical help, my illness had already passed, as I had no choice but to endure it on my own. What makes this particularly disappointing is that I have always had a very positive impression of the Scottish healthcare system. I have travelled here almost every year in the past, and on one occasion I became ill during a trip and received very kind and professional care at a hospital near Leith. Because of that experience, I had a great deal of trust in the healthcare system here. However, after moving here permanently, I have realised that access to care can depend heavily on the individual GP practice you are registered with. Unfortunately, this feels less like a consistent system and more a matter of luck, depending on how well your registered surgery is run. Overall, this experience has been frustrating and has left me feeling disappointed with the level of service provided by this practice.
