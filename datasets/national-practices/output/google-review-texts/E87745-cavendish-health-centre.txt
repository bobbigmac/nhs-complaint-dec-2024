Ongoing relationship approach with gp should be paramount for consideration. There needs to be bilateral trust. There are a few brilliant GPS but you would be fortunate to see them twice. However I have now secured a good ongoing dialogue with the same gp, indicative that they are listening. Iv had excruciating spasms for a month and rarely have to utilise Diazipam but A&e have taken 5 weeks to escalate MRI and Diazipam is antispasmodic and the only effective manner in which to administer in early mornings before arising to reduce spasms, such unjustified control of a vital aid makes for more anxiety and excruciating morning rigidity ......protocol too often can comes before patient need so that is another reason that relationship and understanding of an individual patient is crucial. My husband is 82 and has little sleep having been up helping me 5 times in the night...and having to keep making visits to the chemist as nothing has been effective to date, only Diazipam designed to abate such spasms, no other antispasmodic was discussed. This is now resolved. Cat who was my social provider was a huge help in securing a good counselling option. I don't like to single out individual GPS as it would defeat the object of the review process....The practice manager did send me a compassionate letter this week reaching out and I had a constructve exchange with Tina in pharmacy. my consultant gave the go ahead for diazipam in a letter to Cavendish via my consultant....indicating proactively response albeit taking time. Thank you for addresing all my concerns and there have been many very good experiences.....memorable for the right reasons. A waiting room where I was once offered tea and kindness waiting for an ambulance....it's just been a hellish 6 weeks recently unable to walk or lie down or get in transport but I'm moving now and reassured that my feedback has been valued..
