Author: Rod Wildblood
Date: Edited 3 months ago
Rating: 1 star
Serious concerns about patient safety and complaint handling I am a kidney transplant recipient with a complex medical history, including multiple surgeries and ongoing immunosuppression. In October 2025 I attended the surgery in severe respiratory distress with significant fluid overload, I felt as though I was drowning and had been trying for days to get help. During the consultation the GP listened to my lungs from the back only and declared them “clear”. I was initially refused any treatment or medication because the results of a previous sleep apnoea test were still pending, despite being visibly very unwell. Roughly 30 minutes after I left, the same GP phoned and prescribed antibiotics for collection from Boots, without any further examination or explanation. Very concerned, I immediately contacted my transplant team. My specialist nurse heard obvious abnormalities when listening to my chest. I was sent urgently to James Cook University Hospital where a chest X-ray confirmed pneumonia. I was admitted for almost two weeks, during which I also contracted COVID-19 in hospital. The delay and infection caused significant harm: my creatinine rose to 230 µmol/L (acute kidney injury to my transplant) and I suffered prolonged respiratory distress and lasting lung damage. I submitted a formal complaint through the practice website several weeks before posting my initial Google review and received no acknowledgement at all. Only after I left the public review did the practice send a written response. When I telephoned to discuss it, the operational manager (Amanda Hold) was abrupt, told me the letter was their “final response”, and instructed me to remove this Google review – a request I declined. I have since corrected an earlier mistake in the doctor’s name and apologised for that error; the serious clinical concerns and the practice’s overall handling remain unchanged. As a vulnerable transplant patient, this experience has completely destroyed my confidence in Huntcliff Surgery. I have now escalated the matter to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman in the hope that lessons will be learned and no one else is put at similar risk. R.A.
