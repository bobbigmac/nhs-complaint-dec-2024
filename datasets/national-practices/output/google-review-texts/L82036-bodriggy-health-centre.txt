After 7 days of significant vision changes I called the GP to request an appointment. To even get a phone appointment seemed like a fight with the receptionist suggesting I book an eye test! I felt that I had to plea my case with other symptoms leaning towards diabetes I raised my concern specifically around this and that my mum was a nurse and was again dismissed with an appointment for a CALL in 7 days. I ended up visiting minor injuries who were INCREDIBLE and after some tests confirmed my blood glucose was almost 30 and my blood ketones were at 9, dangerously high. They asked me to go back to my GP for urgent referral and provided as many blood tests as possible to back this up. I then went into the surgery face to face to ask for an emergency appointment and was AGAIN told I could have a call booked for the following day leaving me in tears, I felt like I had to fight my own case to even be heard. I did eventually get a call from the GP later that day who brought me straight in within the hour and gave me tablets for Type 2 diabetes as agreed this was likely what I had until the blood tests came back to confirm. I was then called not my by GP surgery but by MINOR INJURIES the following week who rushed me back in as my HbA1c was at 90 and they were very concerned for my life, they brought me straight in and gave me emergency insulin as well as an appointment at Treliske with the diabetic team the following morning (it was 1800 when I was seen). I was immediately given insulin to bring my BG in line and have since been given Type 1 diagnosis, I didn’t hear from Bodriggy from the moment I walked out of my app and I do wonder if I would have and what would have happened if the MID hadn’t reviewed my blood tests the following week and reacted so quickly. I have since left this surgery as I have been left with no faith that they have patient health at the forefront of their minds. I have also written a letter to the practice manager regarding my experience as if I wasn’t mentally able to advocate for myself I could have continued to live with undiagnosed diabetes and ended up in hospital with DKA.
