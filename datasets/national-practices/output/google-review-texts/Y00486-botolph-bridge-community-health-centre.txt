While I am not disputing the potential clinical quality of the medical staff, I must raise an urgent and serious concern regarding the appointment booking system, which effectively prevents patients from accessing necessary care. THE PHENOMENON OF NON-ACESS: I have attempted to book a GP appointment on two separate occasions this past week by calling precisely at 8.00 am when the lines open: Friday: called exactly at 8.00 am and immediately placed into queue position number 30. Monday: called exactly at 8.00 am. I was again placed into queue position number 30. On both occasions, after holding, I was informed that all available appointments had been allocated. The consistency of reaching queue position 30 immediately at opening time suggests a serious systemic flaw or a statistical anomaly that requires immediate investigation by the practice and the NHS. It is unclear how it is possible for the line to be this busy within milliseconds of opening, making access practically impossible. CONSEQUENCE FOR VULNERABLE PATIENTS: The failure of this system has direct safety implications. As a patient with COPD (a chronic respiratory condition), I was very unwell last week. Because I could not secure an appointment through the standard process, I was forced to contact my hospital nurse who had to intervene and arrange for a doctor from this Surgery to call me back. A primary care service should not require a hospital referral just to secure a basic doctor consultation, especially for high-risk patients. CALL TO ACTION: I urge the practice and the NHS to look into this specific "number 30 phenomenon". The current booking mechanism is failing patients, particularly those with serious health conditions who need timely access. Transparency is needed regarding how the telephone system works and what measures are being taken to ensure fair, prompt access to care at 8.00am.
