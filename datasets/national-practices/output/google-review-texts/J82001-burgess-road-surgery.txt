Reception staff should treat patients with basic courtesy and professionalism. Unfortunately, some receptionists appear to dislike their job, and this attitude can make patients’ experiences unnecessarily unpleasant. When staff behave this way, it can make other people’s lives more difficult and stressful when they are simply trying to access healthcare. Calls are sometimes ended abruptly without even a simple “thank you” or “have a good day,” which reflects poor service. Booking an appointment can also feel as though patients are asking for a favour rather than accessing a service they are entitled to. There is also a recurring issue where receptionists decide whether a patient should see a practitioner or a doctor. Patients are sometimes booked with a practitioner only to be told they actually need a doctor, which wastes both the patient’s time and NHS resources. While one receptionist is always polite and helpful, this level of professionalism should be expected from all staff. Patients deserve respectful communication and an efficient appointment system.
