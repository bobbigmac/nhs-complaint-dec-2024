Dear Practice Manager, I am writing to formally raise a complaint regarding our recent vaccination appointments with Nurse Brenda. As soon as I stepped into the room, I politely asked if I could have five minutes to feed my daughter, Alizeh, as feeding helps soothe her before medical procedures. Nurse Brenda responded, “Oh, not more than five minutes please,” in a dismissive tone. While I understand time constraints, the lack of empathy at that moment was upsetting. During the administration of the rotavirus vaccine, she pushed the oral vaccine into my baby’s mouth very harshly. Alizeh immediately became extremely distressed and started crying intensely. Throughout the appointment, Nurse Brenda referred to my baby as “stubborn,” which I found completely inappropriate and unacceptable when speaking about an infant. Babies are not stubborn—they are scared and uncomfortable—and as parents, we are already anxious during vaccinations. After the vaccine was given, my daughter was very upset. I had to take her out of the room without feeding her properly. Nurse Brenda then added, “Yes, usually infants hate to stay inside my room post vaccination,” which felt dismissive—almost as if to say her job was done and we should leave. I ended up feeding my baby outside the healthcare premises. I would like clarification on whether this is considered appropriate practice. After our first visit, we chose not to leave a review, assuming perhaps she was having a difficult day. Unfortunately, after our second visit, her attitude and behaviour were exactly the same. It is clear this was not a one-off situation. Healthcare professionals working with babies should be calm, kind, patient, and reassuring. Sadly, none of that was present during our experience. The entire situation added unnecessary stress during an already emotional moment for both my baby and our family. I hope this feedback is taken seriously and that appropriate steps are taken to ensure other parents do not have a similar experience. Kind regards, Faiza kokoszka
