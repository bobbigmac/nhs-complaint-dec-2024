Author: Cihan Coşar
Date: a month ago
Rating: 1 star
This review is not about the hospital staff specifically, but about the healthcare system as a whole. Every time I attend A&E, I end up waiting at least 6–7 hours. Despite presenting with the same ongoing issue, I am asked the same questions and undergo the same tests each time, without any real progress toward identifying the root cause. On my first visit, I was told that I needed to wear a Holter monitor to properly diagnose the issue. However, I was informed that such a device was not available in this large hospital. Because my situation was considered urgent, I was told it had been escalated and that I would be contacted within 2–3 days once the device became available. Despite attending A&E on two consecutive days due to urgency, I was sent home each time with reassurance that I would be contacted shortly. One week later, I received a letter by post regarding the Holter monitor appointment — scheduled three months after my initial visit. It is deeply concerning that a diagnostic device considered “urgent” can take three months to access in a major hospital. This experience reflects broader systemic capacity and resource challenges within the NHS. I sincerely hope that greater investment and resource planning can be directed toward essential diagnostic equipment, so that patients with urgent needs can receive timely care.
