I was referred here by my usual GP practice to see Nurse Angela Atkinson. I arrived five minutes late. Five minutes. Instead of handling this professionally or even with basic courtesy, she came into the waiting room and publicly announced that she would not be seeing me because I was late. This was done in front of other patients, with zero discretion and zero empathy. It was unnecessary, embarrassing, and completely avoidable. Patients are routinely expected to wait when clinicians run behind schedule — often 10, 15, even 20 minutes — without explanation or apology. Yet a heavily pregnant patient being five minutes late is apparently unacceptable. The double standard is astonishing. When I calmly questioned the decision, Nurse Atkinson abruptly walked away stating she “needed to go herself anyway.” That comment alone makes it very clear this was not about policy — it was about attitude. Refusing care while casually announcing you’re leaving anyway says everything. The receptionist’s conduct was equally concerning. From the moment I approached the desk, she appeared irritated and dismissive. When I asked to speak to someone else about what had just happened, I was told there was “no one else.” No management, no accountability, no attempt to resolve the situation. Just a wall of indifference. For context, I am heavily pregnant. That was the reason for the appointment. To refuse to see a pregnant patient over a five-minute delay — and to do so publicly — demonstrates a shocking lack of compassion and professionalism. This practice seems to operate on ego rather than patient care. Basic respect costs nothing, yet it was entirely absent. I will be formally escalating this matter.
