Practice response:
We are sorry to hear that you have been disappointed with your experience and understand how frustrating it can be when you are unable to secure an appointment. Our telephone lines open at 8:30am, and appointments are allocated on a first-come, first-served basis. During this busy period, we have 3–4 members of the team answering calls and we offer more than the minimum suggested number of routine appointments. Despite these measures, due to very high demand, all routine appointments are often booked very quickly, and some callers may be advised that no appointments remain. Routine appointments can also be pre-booked in advance through our Weekend Hub clinics, subject to availability, which may offer an alternative option. Where a patient feels their need is urgent, we will always assess this and offer an appropriate alternative to a routine appointment to ensure care is provided safely. We appreciate your feedback and are sorry for the frustration caused. Primary care services are currently under significant pressure, and we continue to do everything possible to meet patient needs within these constraints.

Thank you for taking the time to leave your feedback. We are genuinely sorry to hear about your experience and understand how distressing it is to be without medication. For reasons of confidentiality, we are unable to discuss individual cases on a public platform. If you would like us to look into this further, please contact me directly at sarah.hamson@whitnashmc.nhs.uk and I will be happy to review the situation with you. We would also like to clarify some general information that may be helpful for other patients. Prescriptions for regular medication often require a clinical review before they can be reauthorised. Patients are advised of this in advance and, where concerns about running out of medication are raised with us, we can usually arrange appropriate interim support. Our GPs frequently work well beyond 6.30pm, after the reception team have gone home, in order to manage clinical workload and patient requests. Unfortunately, this means that actions taken later in the evening may not be immediately visible to patients or pharmacies until the next working day. If you are at risk of running out of medication, we strongly encourage patients to: * Contact the surgery to check on the progress of their request * Speak to their regular pharmacist, who may be able to help * Contact **NHS 111**, who can arrange an urgent supply of many medications if appropriate We are committed to supporting our patients, but timely communication and follow-up are essential to ensure we can do so safely. We regret the frustration this situation has caused and welcome direct contact so concerns can be addressed properly.
