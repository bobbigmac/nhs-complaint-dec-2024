So, this isn't against the whole surgery, but my specific GP. The below does contain some patient information, but I am the patient, and I don't care if everyone knows the below. Following treatment for cancer over a decade ago, I still require an annual blood test. For various reasons (mainly continuity of care and a - now totally validated - lack of trust in my GP) I have been having the blood test carried out privately with the oncologist that originally saw me at the start of my treatment. This year, I thought I would try to convert to having these tests carried out by my GP. I don't want to pay for this test every year, and I don't (despite what my Dr once suggested on a ridiculdous phone call) have any loyalty or heroic devotion to my oncologist. Plus, I get an annoying reminder every year that blood tests are required to continue my repeat prescription. (I have separate issues with this process.) I wrote asking my GP to make sure he was confident in carrying out these tests. His reply was (if I'm being kind) brusque. (If I'm being honest, it was condascending.) However, he said yes. So I booked a blood test (after 20 minutes on hold), and once again, checked with the lovely (hence 2 stars, not 1) nurse who was taking my bloods that my Dr was capable of reading, interpretting and indeed even taking the blood tests I needed. His reply was once again a simple (condascending) yes. **Shock of century here** his answer should have been no. Because I totally lacked any confidence in his answer and capabilites, I still went to go see my oncologist. She said I needed to take an additional test (which she emails annually to let him know I need / have taken which he did not request), and low and behold, this test has shown quite a sharp increase in my TgAb levels, which is an indicator of the disease returning. (It could also just be a blip, let's not rush to conclusions.) But the point is, his total lack of care, lack of time, lack of thought, lack of proficiency, could (if it's not a blip) cost me a lot more than five of his minutes time, where he could have easily taken his egotism down a notch and actually looked at the history of the patients under his care. I have requested to change GP, but they are apparently full to capacity and cannot let anyone swap. I understand that everyone is busy, and I am sure (or at least I really hope) that other patients have received better care from my Dr than I have, but this feels like a real slip up that would have been so easy to avoid, especially because I drew his attention to it.
