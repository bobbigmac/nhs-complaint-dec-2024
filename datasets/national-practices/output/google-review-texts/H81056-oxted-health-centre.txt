Author: Mona Frayne-Reid
Date: a month ago
Rating: 1 star
I genuinely appreciate the kindness, professionalism and discretion shown by one or two members of staff — particularly a young receptionist who is consistently respectful and compassionate. Unfortunately, these positive experiences are the exception rather than the rule. Over many years, I have repeatedly experienced dismissive, insensitive and unprofessional behaviour from reception staff that has left me feeling humiliated and unsupported at times when I was already vulnerable. Examples include being spoken to sarcastically when trying to prevent spreading illness, having private medical matters loudly discussed across a full waiting room (including intimate samples despite my request for discretion), and historically being publicly shamed as a teenager when collecting prescribed medication. Patients attend because they are unwell, anxious or frightened — and basic dignity, privacy and empathy should be non-negotiable. Reception is the first point of contact, yet too often interactions feel judgmental, abrupt or lacking in awareness of confidentiality and patient vulnerability. This is particularly concerning given the number of patients managing complex physical and mental health conditions. After years navigating serious health issues, including delayed diagnoses and difficult treatment journeys, I now approach appointments with anxiety rather than reassurance — largely due to front-of-house experiences rather than clinical care. I say this not out of hostility but because standards need to improve. Professionalism, discretion and compassion should be expected at every interaction, not just from a few exceptional individuals.
