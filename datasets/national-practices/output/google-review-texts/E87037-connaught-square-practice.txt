I explained clearly to the doctor that this was a different condition from what I had experienced in the past (which was primarily acne on the body), and that the current symptoms were likely triggered by stress and hormonal or inflammatory changes. However, I felt that the doctor did not listen to or acknowledge my concerns. Instead, he immediately attributed the breakouts to skin picking — an assumption I firmly refuted, as I have been careful not to aggravate the affected areas. When prescribing medication, the doctor asked which treatment had worked best for me in the past. While I responded that a certain tablet had been somewhat effective before, I was confused as to why past treatments for a different type of acne were being used as the basis for the current prescription, given that the symptoms and areas affected are not the same. At one point, I attempted to clarify my uncertainty by saying: “I’m not sure if it’s appropriate to use the same medicine for a different kind of acne, and I’m also not entirely sure about the name of the medicine you’re referring to.” The doctor’s response was dismissive and frankly insulting. He said — and I quote: “You’re not sure about this, not sure about that, what are you even sure about?” This remark made me feel belittled and invalidated at a moment when I was already anxious and vulnerable. I attended the appointment precisely because I needed guidance and support. Instead, I was met with impatience, a lack of empathy, and what I believe to be an unprofessional manner that falls short of the standard of care patients are entitled to under the NHS.
