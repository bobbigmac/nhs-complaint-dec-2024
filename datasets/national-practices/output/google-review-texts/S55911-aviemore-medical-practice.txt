One of the GP Partners wrote on Twitter: "When I tell a patient now that they have cancer the question "should this have been picked up sooner?" seems to be universal #GP #NHS This has nothing to with #NHS and everything to do with the GP partners’ attitudes towards gate-keeping access to diagnostics and specialist care, patients encounter the same gate-keeping problems when they ask for a private referral. Social Media is obviously an inappropriate place to discuss concerns about clinical negligence so the partners need to review the way they respond to and fail to follow-up on formal complaints. Regarding the above, I regret not: 1. saying to the receptionist outright that they cannot refuse to offer an appointment/ examination from a nurse/ doctor - because the symptoms "don't sound 'urgent’" to the receptionist. When you are severely ill, nobody has a right to stop you from seeing a doctor. 2. asking the GP to write down the symptoms I report on the notes including any request for a referral 3. requesting a copy of the medical notes to check for accuracy 4. challenging the GP on refusing to make a referral without doing an appropriate examination 5. checking the clinical guidelines, challenging the GP on their refusal to follow the standard referral pathway 6. taking someone else to the appointment as a witness and advocate 7. Using an online GP service before the appointment, to check which investigations/ examinations the GP should be making and to get further advice. A Digital symptom tracker (Flo) has been more accurate than the diagnoses of the GPs. It is disappointing that even when you use an App that's recommended by the Royal College of GP (RCGP) - like Flo - most of this practice's GPs ignore the information: the problem is the attitude and the arrogance. When the GPs don’t examine their patients to check for a lump and also gate-keep access to urgent diagnostics for patients with a lump, it is not surprising that the question "should (cancer) have been picked up sooner?" seems to be a universal question among the practices' patients.
