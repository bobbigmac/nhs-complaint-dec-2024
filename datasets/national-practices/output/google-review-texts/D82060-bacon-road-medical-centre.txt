Practice response date: 3 days ago
Practice response:
Thank you for your feedback, though several of the claims you have made are not accurate. Online access is available, but—like all GP practices—we must follow national clinical‑triage requirements. Patients cannot self‑book directly into GP appointments without any clinical information, because this would be unsafe and would disadvantage those with urgent needs. This is a national model, not a local preference. Your claim that practices are “paid thousands extra” for online booking is incorrect. The contractual changes relating to digital access do not take effect until 1 April 2026, and even those changes do not provide additional payments in the way you suggest. They are amendments to the national GP contract, not optional paid add‑ons. They are also currently being formally challenged by the BMA, which has raised concerns about both the feasibility and safety impact of those requirements. In other words, the position is far more complex than your statement implies. Our telephone system also does not “hang up” on callers. It uses a live queue, but if the queue becomes so large that it cannot be answered safely—such as near closing time, or during short periods when staffing is reduced at lunchtime—the system will inform callers and advise them to call back later. This prevents long waits that could not realistically be answered. Reception staff do not “grill” patients. They ask brief triage questions because clinicians require this information to determine urgency and route patients to the most appropriate member of the multidisciplinary team. This is standard NHS practice and is essential for safe care. We hope this provides some clarity.
