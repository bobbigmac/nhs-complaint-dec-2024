I do not recommend if you have gender dysphoria or ADHD. Practice response date: a month ago
Practice response:
Dear Art, Thank you for sharing your feedback. We sincerely understand the frustration and anxiety that navigating ADHD and gender dysphoria care can cause, and we regret that you feel our communication has been inconsistent. As a partnership, our primary responsibility is patient safety. While 'Right to Choose' (RTC) allows patients to access assessments through various NHS-commissioned providers, 'Shared Care'—where a GP takes over the prescribing of specialist medication—is a voluntary agreement. Our practice policy aligns with most other practices across Birmingham and Solihull (BSOL). We have made the clinical decision to only enter Shared Care agreements through established local BSOL pathways. This is to ensure that the specialist oversight, long-term physical monitoring, and emergency support meet the specific safety standards required in our region. It is important to clarify that if a GP practice is unable to accept a Shared Care agreement, the specialist provider remains clinically and contractually responsible for the patient's ongoing care. This includes the continued issuing of prescriptions. Our decision does not stop your treatment; rather, it ensures that the specialist who initiated your care remains the one managing it. This avoids the need for patients to pay privately, as the RTC provider is NHS-funded for this purpose. We aim for clarity and safety in all our clinical decisions. If you would like to discuss your specific circumstances or how we can support your transition to a local pathway, please contact us. Kind Regards Dan Hill (Managing Partner)
