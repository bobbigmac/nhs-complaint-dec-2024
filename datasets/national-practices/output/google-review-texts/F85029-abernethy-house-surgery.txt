Author: Phil T

Rating: 1 star
The motto of Abernethy House is "Whatever you do, don't let anybody see the doctors!" I posted an original review (below) a few months ago. I then removed it in the interests of fairness. I accepted that maybe my experience was a one-off; just unfortunate: not representative of what normally happens. I also managed to see a doctor while I was abroad (I know right! Isn't that strange behaviour Abernethy House? A patient with a condition that needs to be diagnosed? The surgery provides a time and date for an appointment. He visits the surgery on the specified date and gets to see a doctor. Crazy behaviour! It must be because they're foreign. They probably eat weird food too). Well, regrettably I had no choice but to try again. Can you guess what happened? I get exactly the same obstructive practices as before. I have to waste a silly amount of time filling in a useless form that always produces the same result - "don't bother us. Whatever the condition, we'll palm you off to 111 or the hospital." Abernethy House's motto still holds true. Original Post: NHS resources are of course limited, and different surgeries operate different approaches to managing this. Abernethy House's philosophy can be summarised as "use as many obstacles as possible to prevent people from seeing a doctor." If you wish to make an appointment, they make you fill in a lengthy online form. It requires you to select specific options and try to explain your symptoms. Here's the clever part. It will turn you away (from getting an appointment with a GP) at every possible opportunity. No matter what variation or in which order I described my symptoms, the response was the same - no doctor for you. You must go to the hospital or use 111. For the record, both options were not appropriate. It wasn't an emergency and 111 would be unable to help. Nonetheless, I tried 111 (knowing full well that it would tell me to make a GP's appointment). With 111, you have three options; an app, a phone call or their online service. I chose the online method. You'll be amazed to learn that it informed me to make a GP's appointment! I therefore returned to the dreaded eConsult form. By spending (a significant amount of) time and rejigging my responses, I finally managed to submit a request for an appointment (having been advised numerous times to use 111). I was later called by the surgery. They informed me that 111 had no record of my call, and they therefore couldn't give me an appointment. I replied that I used their online service and it advised me to make a GP's appointment. Let's briefly discuss 111. Its purpose is to help those people who have a medical problem that doesn't equate to a 999 emergency. You can use it when your GP surgery is out of hours. It IS NOT a vetting service for doctor's surgeries to redirect people to. It should not be used as an obstacle to seeing your doctor and Abernethy House should be thoroughly ashamed for misusing it this way. Furthermore, if I do use 111, it should be my choice to determine which of the three methods I use. What's particularly frustrating is the hubris you'll find when reading the website. "Overall, this system has allowed us to better manage the appointments." (It) "has improved the efficient use of all available clinical and administrative resources." Yes, I'm 100% sure the system has allowed you to better manage the appointments. Just like the trains would be much easier to manage if the operators prevented you from buying a ticket. The roads would have far less congestion if the DVLA refused to issue driving licenses. Abernethy House is a doctor's surgery that won't let you see a doctor. Of course it's running better.
