Author: Naomi Summerscales
Date: Edited 2 months ago
Rating: 1 star
In the past 2 weeks I have tried to get my son seen for 2 separate reasons. The first was a very large bump on his forehead, which resulted in an egg shaped lump. I walked him over immediately to see if I could get him seen by a nurse or doctor to check he was ok and was told that (despite I had no transport) I had to take him to A&E in Kingsbridge to get advice. They refused to let him see a doctor even for a quick once over. Then today, I called up and explained that he has sores all over his lips and mouth making it very painful and I wanted him to see a doctor and again they refused to let him be seen by a doctor. This time I was told we had to take him to Kingsbridge to see a pharmacist. When I said, “what if they just say he needs to see a doctor”, they said “well he can see one then”. If they could see his face they would understand why he needs a doctor - but they didn’t even ask for photos. This system is just passing the buck and as a member of the surgery it feels totally unfair that you can not see a local doctor but instead have to travel 20 mins into Kingsbridge to see a pharmacist. In terms of looking out for the local community this isn’t a good enough service. As a member of this surgery it feels like a lottery system as to whether you get to see an actual doctor or not. This is anxiety inducing. I’m very, very disappointed.
Practice response date: 3 months ago
Practice response:
Thank you for taking the time to leave your feedback Naomi. We’re very sorry to hear how stressful these situations have been for you — it’s completely understandable to feel anxious when your child is unwell. Regarding your first concern, we would like to reassure you that the advice given about your son’s head injury was in line with NHS guidance. Large bumps or “egg-shaped” swellings in children should be assessed in A&E, where the right equipment is available to rule out more serious injury. Although frustrating, especially without transport, this was the safest route for your son at the time. For your more recent call about lip and mouth sores, the advice to see a pharmacist is generally appropriate under the NHS “Pharmacy First” service. However, based on what you described and the fact that you had no transport, we agree that an alternative option — such as sending photographs, completing an eConsult or arranging a duty clinician to review — should have been considered. We are sorry this wasn’t offered, and we will follow this up with the team so that these options are consistently discussed in future. We would be happy to look into your concerns in more detail and ensure you feel supported going forward. As we cannot discuss personal information publicly, if you would like to contact our Operations Lead, Gemma Morris, on enquiries.chillington@nhs.net or 01548 580214 we can review this fully. Thank you again for sharing your experience — your feedback genuinely helps us improve the service we provide to our community. Kind regards, Jade Greet Practice Manager
