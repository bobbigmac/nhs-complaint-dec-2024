We have been very patient patients with this surgery for the best part of three years. But they keep falling short of my very minimal expectations. I had a blood test a few months ago to see if I needed to increase my dose of Allopurinol-not heard anything. They asked me to monitor my BP for a week, as I was borderline hypertensive, and nothing. No correspondence. My other half did the same-no follow up either. We work in the NHS too, so we understand all about the pressures-and when it’s your colleagues that are failing you, it is extremely disappointing and frustrating. Unfortunately, our last experience is trying to get a repeat prescription for our son’s ADHD medication called Clonidine on the 10th of November. This for me, is the last straw. They didn’t mention that we needed to do anything else until we called them back a week later to see if the prescription is ready, only to be told that they need a letter from CAMHS, which tells us that clearly, there was no communication between the two. CAMHS isn’t the best too, in terms of follow-ups. But that’s another story. Now we are down to a day’s worth of Clonidine-a drug that you cannot stop abruptly because of potential hypertensive complications. The GPs should know that. It’s as if they couldn’t care less what happens to the patient if they don’t get the medication on time. And these people took the Hippocratic oath-to do no harm.
