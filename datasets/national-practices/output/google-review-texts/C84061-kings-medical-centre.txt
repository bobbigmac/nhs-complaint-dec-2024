I had an appointment at GP Room 2 because I have been waking up at least three or four times a night to go to the bathroom, and sometimes during the day almost every hour. The GP prescribed treatment, but it didn’t help, so I was given a yellow card to book blood tests at reception to check my blood sugar levels. I booked and attended the appointment, but when I arrived, the nurse said she didn’t know what the tests were for — even though I had already seen her before. I explained that my GP had asked for blood sugar tests and that it might not have been recorded properly. She eventually took my blood, but with some difficulty. A few days later, when I received my results, I realised that the wrong tests had been done. I called reception to explain what had happened. The receptionist said she would speak to a GP and call me back to reschedule the correct blood tests. I was very frustrated because it meant missing another day of work. Today, I had a second appointment at 7:30. When I arrived at Room 9, there were access issues, and once again, the nurse said she couldn’t take my blood because she didn’t know what the tests were for. I explained what the GP had said, but she began raising her voice at me, saying she couldn’t take the sample until she spoke to the GP. I asked her to please take my blood first and confirm later, as I couldn’t afford to lose another day. I’m very disappointed that I’ve now lost two days for nothing because of repeated miscommunication. I work full-time, have three children, and my time is limited. It’s frustrating to be called in for tests that can’t be completed due to errors on their side. Is it really so difficult to organise blood tests properly? Why didn’t anyone call me to inform me not to come if the GP hadn’t provided enough details? This isn’t the first time this has happened, and both I and my family are very disappointed with the service at Kings Medical Centre.
