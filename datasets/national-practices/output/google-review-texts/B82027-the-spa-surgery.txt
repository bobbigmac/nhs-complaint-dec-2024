I’m not actually a patient with this surgery but my Grandma is. I called the other day and spoke to a young girl on the phone trying to book an appointment for my Grandma, she was unwell with a number of things. The young girl I spoke to was very well mannered, kind and understanding. The information was “passed to the duty team” a process I found confusing as I felt she needed to be seen in person. I queried this and the young girl explained they use a ‘triage’ system, so we’d get a text with an appointment and that the reception team no longer book emergency appointments. It makes total sense to do it this way as I’d sooner a doctor decide how quickly my grandmother is to be seen than a receptionist. We got a message, as promised, within 10 minutes inviting us for an appointment within 2 hours. The doctor we saw (I couldn’t catch the name but I think it may have been Dr. Ganeeshi) was brilliant, caring kind and dealt with my frail Grandma very well. We then had to book some bloods and collect some sample pots, so we popped back to reception and spoke to a young man called Ben. It was refreshing to see a young man working reception and he himself dealt with my grandmother with a sense of patience (she often gets confused) and care, his colleague who I believe was called Beth was on the other side of the desk and I think it may have been her I spoke to on the phone earlier in the day. Both receptionists had a great deal of empathy both with my grandmother and each patient (there were many) I saw them deal with. It’s incredibly refreshing to see such lovely receptionists, being able to have both tricky and positive interactions, it’s rare you hear of GP receptionists smiling! But it’s clear they both care a great deal about what they do. Based on all of this, I may become a Spa Surgery patient myself! Thank you all for your care and kindness, even with the NHS the way it is you all continue to be so supportive. My only gripe? Please inform your patients if you change systems. The ‘triage’ system works, but you didn’t tell anyone about the change.
