Author: Alex Marginean
Date: 3 weeks ago
Rating: 2 stars
I recently joined this GP surgery and unfortunately my experience has been quite stressful, particularly at a time when I am already struggling with my mental health. My psychiatrist advised that he would issue documentation to support a sick note extension and informed me that I should be able to contact my GP the same day (Friday) initially to arrange a one week extension (given that I've already self-certified for a week), with the option to review again and extend more if needed once his letter was processed the following week. However, when I called reception, I received significant pushback. I was told it would be “illegal” to issue a sick note without being seen first and that I would need to book a routine appointment, with the earliest availability in two weeks. This was despite my mental health history being documented on my records and the psychiatrist’s supporting note pending. I explained that this situation was worsening my anxiety and that I felt very uncomfortable being off work without formal documentation. I was told the note could be issued retrospectively, but the process still felt unnecessarily difficult and dismissive. After persistent requests, I was advised to call back once I had the psychiatrist’s letter and potentially attend the surgery in person to have it scanned, at which point they would “see if” a doctor could review it sooner. I also asked whether I could speak to a duty doctor or have an on-the-day appointment, but was told this is not something they offer for sick notes. After discussing this with others and researching standard practice at GP surgeries, I understand that many surgeries allow patients to submit an e-consult or speak with a duty doctor for matters like this, especially when supporting documentation from a specialist is being provided. This left me feeling unsupported. This is not the first difficulty I’ve experienced with this surgery. Booking appointments is consistently challenging. Routine appointments are limited to a short booking window, phone appointments cannot be arranged online and require calling at specific times, and there is no email contact because, as explained to me, they stopped it due to “too many emails.” Overall, the system feels inaccessible and unnecessarily rigid.
