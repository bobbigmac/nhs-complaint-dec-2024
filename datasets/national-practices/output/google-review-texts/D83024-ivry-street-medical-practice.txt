Headline: Exceptional individual care marred by inconsistency and professionalism gaps. ​My experience with Ivory Street Medical Centre has been a 50/50 mix. On one hand, there are staff members who are "above and beyond." Specifically, the nurse responsible for blood tests and heart rate monitoring is excellent—thorough, professional, and highly recommended. The reception team is generally helpful, and some doctors provide a great standard of care. ​However, there are significant issues regarding professional standards and "lazy" diagnostic assumptions: ​Diagnostic Tunnel Vision: I had a health assessment where breathing issues were dismissed almost entirely as "anxiety." Despite my attempts to explain other factors, my input was ignored. It has since been confirmed that there were other sources for these symptoms beyond just anxiety. ​Professionalism & Accountability: I have encountered staff who openly admitted to being "under-qualified" for a standard request after I had waited a month for the appointment. To make matters worse, I felt mocked when raising concerns, and my attempts to complain were met with denials or being called a liar by the individuals involved. ​Communication Failures: My social prescriber requested a psychiatric evaluation based on a misunderstanding of my needs, yet admitted she does not read the emails sent to her—despite providing the email address herself and asking for contact via that method. ​It is frustrating to wait a month for an appointment only to be told the professional cannot help and must refer you elsewhere, leading to another month of waiting for a "reasonable request." While the good staff are great, the lack of professional accountability and the tendency to default to mental health labels rather than listening to the patient is a major concern.

Author: Andreea Dumitrescu

I am currently 8 weeks pregnant and self-referred for antenatal care in early September. At my GP appointment on 22 September, I was told that if I hadn’t heard back from the midwives, the practice would chase the referral on my behalf. Today (14 October), I followed up exactly as instructed. The receptionist dismissed my concern, stating that booking appointments happen at 12 weeks and that they “can’t do anything.” This directly contradicts both NHS guidance and the advice I received from the GP. For reference, the official NHS website clearly states that the first midwife (booking) appointment should take place between 8 and 10 weeks, not 12. By providing incorrect information, your staff risk delaying essential blood tests and early screening potentially putting expectant mothers at risk. The attitude and misinformation I experienced demonstrate a serious lack of training and empathy from the reception team. It’s not acceptable to tell a pregnant woman misleading information when the NHS’s own published advice says otherwise. I strongly urge the practice to review its staff training and ensure receptionists understand the basic antenatal care timeline. Expectant mothers deserve accurate information and proactive support, not dismissive and uninformed responses.
