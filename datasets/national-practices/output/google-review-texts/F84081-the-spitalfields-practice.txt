Practice response date: 2 weeks ago
Practice response:
Thank you for bringing this matter to our attention. We would like to sincerely apologise for the distress and concern this incident has caused you and others who received the email. We understand how worrying it is to receive an unexpected communication and to see email addresses visible to other recipients. Protecting personal information is extremely important, and we fully appreciate the seriousness of your concerns. We would like to clarify that this communication was not sent directly by The Spitalfields Practice. It was issued by our Primary Care Network (PCN), which manages certain communications across practices within the local catchment area. On 25 February, a member of the PCN team sent a bulk email inviting patients to sign up to the GetUBetter app. Unfortunately, the email was sent using the “To” field rather than BCC, which meant recipient email addresses were visible to others. As soon as the issue was identified, the PCN took immediate action: They attempted to recall the email (with support from NEL IT and the NHSmail Helpdesk, although recall was not possible). A follow-up apology email was sent, asking recipients to delete the original message and not to forward it. The incident was formally logged and documented internally. A formal risk assessment has been initiated by the PCN Data Protection Officer (DPO) to determine whether the threshold for reporting to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) has been met. Preventative measures are being implemented, including reinforcing guidance to staff on the correct use of BCC for bulk communications and refresher Information Governance training. Importantly, no clinical information or special category data was included in the email. The only information disclosed was recipient email addresses. There is currently no indication that any further personal or medical information was shared. We also understand your concern about why you may have received this email if you are not registered with our practice. The PCN holds and manages communication lists across its network, and this is being reviewed as part of the investigation to ensure that distribution lists are accurate and appropriate. We are very sorry that this has happened and for the frustration it has caused. Please be assured that the matter is being taken seriously at PCN level, and steps are being taken to reduce the risk of this happening again. If you would like to raise your concerns formally or require further information about the investigation, please contact the PCN directly via their Data Protection Officer. We are also happy to help direct your query to the appropriate contact if needed. Once again, please accept our sincere apologies for the concern this incident has caused.
