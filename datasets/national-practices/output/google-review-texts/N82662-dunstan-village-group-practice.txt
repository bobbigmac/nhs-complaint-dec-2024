I am extremely disappointed with both the clinical handling and administrative management at this practice, not just recently but over a sustained period of time. Most recently, I sought help for a persistent and worsening cough lasting over five weeks, accompanied by nasal blockage, chesty and tickly coughing fits, sore throat, and episodes severe enough to cause nausea and nosebleeds. Despite the duration and escalation of symptoms, I was never clinically examined. I was referred by the GP surgery to a pharmacy for antibiotics. After completing the pharmacy assessment, I was told they could not issue antibiotics or any treatment and that I would need a GP prescription. When I returned to the surgery and explained this clearly, I was again refused an appointment and told the practice was fully booked and that I should either call back another day or attend a walk-in centre. This resulted in an unacceptable situation where no professional took responsibility for my care. I would also like to make it clear that I am a working individual, I work full time, pay my taxes, and pay for my prescriptions. I have never claimed benefits and I do not attend the GP unnecessarily. I only seek medical help when it is genuinely needed. I am not at this practice frequently, and I do not expect special treatment — I simply expect to be seen and assessed when my health clearly requires it. In addition to this incident, I have experienced ongoing problems with appointment management at this practice. On several occasions in the past, appointments I booked were later changed or cancelled by the surgery due to doctor availability or internal scheduling. When an appointment is cancelled and moved to another day, it is not always possible to rearrange work and responsibilities at short notice. Patients are not machines that can repeatedly reorganise their lives without consequence. Communication from reception staff has consistently been poor. Interactions feel rigid and procedural, with little evidence of judgement, discretion, or escalation to a clinician. The default response often appears to be refusal rather than problem-solving. This has been my experience both at the medical centre reception and during the pharmacy referral process. The overall impression is of a system that prioritises deflection over assessment, where genuine concerns are dismissed and responsibility is passed between services. This is not patient-centred care, and it is not acceptable. A cough lasting longer than three weeks, particularly when worsening, is widely recognised as requiring proper medical assessment. Being passed between services without examination or resolution is unsafe. As a result of these repeated experiences, I have lost confidence in this practice’s ability to provide timely, fair, and responsible care.
