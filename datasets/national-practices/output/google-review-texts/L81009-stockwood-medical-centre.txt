1 Star – A System Focused on Managing Pain, Not Fixing It In 2014, I went to my GP with upper back pain and tingling in my arms. I was a self-employed carpenter with 20 years in the trade. My body was my livelihood. What followed was nearly a decade of chronic pain — and a treatment pathway that focused almost entirely on symptom management. The default response was painkillers. When those didn’t work, stronger painkillers. Then steroid injections. At no point was I given a long-term, structured rehabilitation plan focused on correcting posture, muscular imbalance, or movement dysfunction — despite this clearly being a musculoskeletal issue. I don’t believe the individuals I saw lacked care. The problem felt systemic. The protocol seems built around managing pain quickly and cheaply in the short term, rather than investing in proper rehabilitation that could prevent long-term suffering. Eventually, scans showed rotator cuff tendon impingements, tendinopathies, and C7 foraminal cervical stenosis. Yet even with clear findings, the approach didn’t shift toward comprehensive rehabilitation. Over time, I lost my ability to work. I had to give up my 20-year career as a carpenter because the pain became unmanageable. Years of medication also left me with ongoing stomach problems that I still deal with today. What troubles me most is this: musculoskeletal conditions are often treatable with structured, progressive rehabilitation. But GPs are general practitioners — they are not trained rehabilitation specialists. Without deeper biomechanical assessment, issues like postural dysfunction and muscle imbalance can be missed entirely. Out of desperation, I retrained and studied rehabilitation myself. Through targeted strength training, correcting movement patterns, and addressing long-standing imbalances, I rebuilt my body. Today, I am no longer living in chronic upper body pain. That tells me the issue wasn’t that my condition was untreatable — it was that the system wasn’t set up to treat it properly. My hope is that musculoskeletal protocols within the NHS evolve to prioritise early, structured rehabilitation over long-term reliance on medication and injections. It would save careers. It would save quality of life. And in the long run, it would likely save public money too. No one should lose their livelihood because the system defaults to managing symptoms instead of correcting causes. If you’re living with ongoing upper back pain, shoulder pain, or tingling in your arms — and you feel like you’re only being offered temporary fixes — please don’t wait ten years like I did. Chronic pain is not something you just have to “manage.” In many cases, it can be understood, addressed, and improved through proper assessment, structured rehabilitation, and correcting muscular imbalances. At Preservation Fitness Bristol, my mission is simple: To help hardworking people rebuild their bodies properly — not just mask symptoms. I understand what it feels like to lose your strength, your confidence, and your livelihood because of pain. That’s why my approach focuses on: Identifying the root cause Correcting posture and movement dysfunction Rebuilding strength progressively Providing ongoing support and guidance If you’re ready to take control of your recovery instead of relying solely on pain relief, reach out. Don’t let chronic pain define the next decade of your life.
