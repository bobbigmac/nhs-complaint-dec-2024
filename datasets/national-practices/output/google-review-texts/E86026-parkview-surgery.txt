I registered with this GP surgery in 2013, and I have not been registered with them for several years now. However, I feel that I should share my experience to warn others. My first bad experience was with the receptionist. At the time, my English was not very good. I went to the surgery in person to ask for an appointment. I didn’t fully understand what she said, so I politely asked if she could write it down for me. She looked at me and said directly to my face, “Why do you come here if you don’t speak English?” I found this comment extremely rude, humiliating, and discriminatory. Another serious incident happened when I had a throat infection with a high temperature and could barely speak. I went to the surgery asking for an emergency appointment. The receptionist told me there were no emergency appointments available and wrote down an address where she said I could go over the weekend because that GP would be open. The address she gave me turned out to be a veterinary clinic. I was shocked and very upset, especially given how unwell I was at the time. I also had very negative experiences with Dr Stera. On multiple occasions, I felt rushed and dismissed. Even when I booked a double appointment, I was seen for less than five minutes and felt pushed out without my concerns being properly addressed. At one appointment, I asked to change my birth control because I had started gaining weight rapidly. Instead of discussing it properly, she told me to “just eat less.” When I explained that my eating habits had not changed and that the weight gain started after beginning the contraceptive, she dismissed my concerns and said birth control does not cause weight gain — even though the patient information leaflet clearly lists weight gain as a possible side effect. I felt completely unheard and gaslighted. I also tried to help a friend register at this GP surgery as it was the closest. They refused to register him because he did not have proof of address. I informed them that the NHS website clearly states that patients should not be refused registration due to lack of proof of address, and that even homeless individuals have the right to register with a GP. Despite this, they still refused. Only after I made an official complaint did they finally register him. I should not have had to go that far for them to follow NHS guidance. If I had known earlier how to make a formal complaint, I would have done so after my very first bad experience. Unfortunately, at the time I did not know the proper process, and the behaviour continued. Overall, my experience with this surgery left me feeling disrespected, dismissed, and not taken seriously as a patient. I expected much better care, professionalism, and basic respect.
Practice response date: a month ago
Practice response:
Hi Romola, I would like to start by apologising that you had such a negative experience in 2013 when you were a patient with us and felt you were unable to address this with us at the time. However, I am unable to comment on behalf of the team from 2013. What I can say is that our team has changed dramatically over the past 10+ years and is not the same team you once knew. When it comes to registering new patients at the surgery, we sometimes have difficulty locating an NHS number if it has not been provided. In these cases, we may ask for identification to verify correct spellings of names and dates of birth, for example. If an individual does not have ID, we may then ask whether proof of address can be provided. I am sorry if this came across as us refusing registration, as you are correct that no GP surgery can refuse registration due to a lack of proof of address. Our intention is simply to ensure the details are correct. If a patient is registered with incorrect information, this can result in a new NHS number being generated, which can cause significant issues with the handling of medical records. We value feedback and I hope this explanation provides some clarity.
