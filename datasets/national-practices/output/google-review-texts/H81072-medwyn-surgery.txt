Author: Aura
Date: 2 months ago
Rating: 3 stars
This is a confusing place to navigate. We go to a GP practice for health, yet communication here can feel difficult, especially for autistic patients. I’m not alone in noticing this. Being “CQC approved” doesn’t automatically mean patients feel understood or met where they are. I want to be clear: I’ve also had genuinely positive experiences here, to the point that I transferred back. This is not a one-star review. What’s challenging is the level of stress my body experiences in relation to this practice. Health care should support nervous-system regulation, not add to dysregulation. I often leave feeling unseen, as though patients are expected to fit a standard system rather than being met as individuals. Communication can feel inconsistent. Sometimes calls come without notice, while messages can take time to be answered. At times, it feels as though you’re required to go against your nature’s way in order to meet the system, rather than the system meeting you halfway. When you’re dealing with ongoing health needs, that mismatch can be deeply unsettling. Everyone is a human being with a story. Everyone deserves to feel equally worthy of care. At times, it feels as though patients are expected to adapt to the system rather than the system adapting to the human in front of them. I don’t believe this comes from bad intent. I see it as part of a wider issue in healthcare, where nervous-system awareness and individual needs are not always prioritised. This practice can be helpful. And at the same time, it can feel emotionally distant. A bit like a parent who helps you, but doesn’t always see or communicate in the way you need. Meeting patients halfway, as human beings first, would make a meaningful difference.
