Author: Alex Norman
Date: 7 months ago
Rating: 1 star
We rented through this agency for a year in 251 Southwark Bridge Road and had an exceptionally poor experience before, during and after the tenancy. Prior to the tenancy the agent tried to change the tenancy terms after they had been confirmed and very shortly before we were due to move, which in hindsight should have been a red flag and should have caused us to walk away. Unfortunately, given the timing, we had little choice but to proceed. The apartment was handed over to us in poor condition, with claims by the agency that certain professional cleaning had occurred (e.g. cleaning of carpets prior to check-in) being demonstrably false to the naked eye. There were also multiple basic repairs (broken blinds, smoke alarms not working properly etc) which should have been completed before we moved in and which took weeks for the agency to fully resolve. During the tenancy the agency dragged its feet on repairs. This included essential repairs to the heating mechanism, which we were told by an engineer was effectively costing us unnecessary money in heating bills as a result of the system being in disrepair. After the tenancy the landlord attempted to make a number of spurious claims against the deposit for cleaning and redecoration. Happily for us, we had appropriate records, which led to the Tenancy Deposit Service ruling in our favour and returning the full deposit to us. The process of resolving the deposit dispute was made more difficult by the unreasonable and combative attitude of the agency staff. The process was painful and prolonged and the right outcome was only reached as a result of our own diligence and record keeping. My advice to anyone forced to rent through this agency is to deal with the agency on the assumption they will act unscrupulously, and to protect yourself accordingly. In particular, tenants who have to use them would be well advised to: - Keep very good records (especially of all defects and condition on check-in and check-out, including photos and time stamped emails etc) and be prepared to use them to defend yourself if needed. - Ensure that the reports provided by the agent's inventory clerks reflect the actual state of the property and keep records of where you send comments on those reports. In our case, the agency submitted an inventory report to the Tenancy Deposit Service which didn't include our comments, and we were thankful for our diligence in preserving our comments so we could correct this with the TDS. - Notify the agency immediately and in full in writing of any and all defects, even if you think they are minor or inconsequential. - Put all verbal assurances given by the agency in writing, to avoid the agency claiming the assurances were never given. Having seen largely poor reviews of this agency on Google before the start of our tenancy, I was surprised to see they have a reasonably good Google rating when I came to write this review. On closer inspection, it was less surprising that many of the positive reviews were posted from accounts with (un)surprisingly small numbers of other reviews and who often posted them within the same time period a little over 11 months ago. I would suggest prospective tenants keep this in mind when digesting the other reviews and any agency response to this one.
