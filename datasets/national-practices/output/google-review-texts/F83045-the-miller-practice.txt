Dear Sabs Sky, Thank you for taking the time to share your experience, and we are truly sorry for the frustration and concern this situation has caused—especially given that you are pregnant and caring for a young child. Our usual waiting time for routine GP appointments is around two weeks, but over the past month we have experienced an unusually high level of demand, which has unfortunately pushed our waiting times up further. We are actively working to bring this back down, including bringing in additional clinical staff. For urgent concerns, we do have a duty doctor (often two) available each day from 08:30 to 18:30. We are very sorry if this option was not clearly explained to you at the time. In terms of iHUB, this is a service funded by local GP practices to help support patient care during periods of high demand. While we appreciate it may not always be as convenient as being seen at your registered practice, it is intended to ensure patients can still access timely appointments when we are particularly busy. We are really sorry that your experience did not reflect the level of care we aim to provide, and we genuinely appreciate you bringing this to our attention. Practice response:
Dear Stevie, Thank you for taking the time to share your feedback. We’re very sorry to hear how distressing and debilitating your symptoms have been, and we understand how frustrating it can feel when you believe a specialist referral is needed. We would like to clarify that referrals to secondary care, including neurology, must meet specific clinical criteria set by local NHS pathways. Unfortunately, referrals that do not meet these criteria are often rejected by the specialist service and returned to the GP practice, which can delay care further and cause additional frustration for patients. Our GPs are required to follow these guidelines and to ensure that all appropriate assessments, investigations, and initial management steps have been completed before making a referral. This is not a reflection of the seriousness of a patient’s symptoms, but rather a necessary part of ensuring referrals are accepted and that patients receive the most appropriate care as efficiently as possible. We strongly encourage you to book a further appointment with one of our clinicians to review your symptoms, discuss any changes or progression, and explore the next appropriate steps in your care. If you would prefer, you can also ask about seeking a second opinion within the practice. We genuinely want to support you and help you access the right care, and we’re sorry that this has been such a difficult experience for you. Best wishes, Dr. James Hibberd (GP Partner)
