I am writing to formally express my serious concern regarding the care my 3-month-old baby received during our recent appointment. My infant had been unwell for over a week, with symptoms worsening day by day. Despite administering paracetamol as advised, there was no improvement. At the time of the appointment, my baby was struggling to breathe and unable to breastfeed properly. As a parent, this was extremely alarming, and I sought medical care because I was genuinely worried about my child’s safety. Unfortunately, the consultation was deeply disappointing and distressing. Rather than conducting a thorough assessment or acknowledging the seriousness of the symptoms in such a young infant, the doctor repeatedly advised me to “wait a few more days” and return only if the condition did not improve. This advice was given despite clear signs of deterioration and despite the fact that my baby is only 3 months old. I also felt spoken to in a dismissive and condescending manner, as though my concerns were based on a lack of understanding rather than careful observation of my own child. Parents are often the first to notice when something is wrong, especially with infants who cannot communicate their distress. Being made to feel disregarded while my baby was visibly unwell was extremely upsetting. For an infant of this age, difficulty breathing and feeding are well-known red flags that require prompt and careful evaluation. A “wait and see” approach in this situation felt unsafe and inappropriate. I left the appointment feeling unheard, unsupported, and worried that my baby’s condition was not taken seriously. I am providing this feedback not only because of my own experience, but out of concern for other families who may be in similar situations. I hope this is taken seriously and used to reflect on clinical decision-making and communication with parents, particularly when caring for very young and vulnerable patients.
