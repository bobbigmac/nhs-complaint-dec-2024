My experience with the administrative team at this GP surgery has been extremely distressing and deeply concerning. I requested a simple medical letter confirming my conditions and medications on official letterhead. Instead of providing the letter requested, I was sent a basic extract from the NHS app containing incorrect information, missing conditions, and even my recent case notes. Including private case notes in a document that was not requested raises serious concerns about patient confidentiality and potential GDPR breaches. More than two weeks later, the actual letter has still not been produced, despite being told it would be ready last week. I am still waiting. Communication with the reception/admin team has been consistently poor: • Staff do not introduce themselves when answering the phone, so patients have no idea who they are speaking to. • Different staff members provide conflicting information about the same request. • The tone is frequently hostile, dismissive and unprofessional. • On one occasion my call was hung up on after I expressed frustration about the delays and errors. For context, my medical conditions are significantly triggered by stress and anxiety, and unfortunately every time I have to contact this surgery I feel a high level of anxiety because of how hostile and confrontational the interactions often are. During the call that was terminated, I was expressing frustration about a simple administrative request that has still not been completed, but this was wrongly interpreted as “shouting”. There appears to be little ability to differentiate between a frustrated patient and inappropriate behaviour, which only escalates already stressful situations. What is particularly worrying is the lack of basic competence in handling medical administrative tasks. Reception staff even did not recognise the name of a senior doctor at the practice, which I had to spell out for them. This practice has previously been taken into administration, which unfortunately makes the current level of disorganisation less surprising. However, under the current management, the administrative service appears even worse than before. The issues described raise serious concerns about patient care standards, confidentiality, and administrative competence. Patients who have similar experiences may wish to raise concerns with the Care Quality Commission, which regulates healthcare services. I will also be raising my own concerns with both the Care Quality Commission and NHS England regarding these issues. Patients deserve respectful communication, competent administration, and proper handling of confidential medical information. Unfortunately, my experience with this surgery has demonstrated the opposite.
