I had an appointment at 10:50am for my flu vaccination. Unfortunately, there was a power cut at the time (which I completely understand was not the practice’s fault). The first receptionist I spoke to was lovely — she apologised and explained I could either wait or return at 1pm. I chose to come back later. At 12pm, I called to check whether the power was back on to avoid waiting unnecessarily. When I arrived shortly after, I was told by a red-haired receptionist that I was supposed to be there at 12. I explained that I had been told 1pm. She then told me to go upstairs and wait for the nurse. Shortly after, she came upstairs and informed me that I was not entitled to receive a flu vaccination. This was confusing, as I had one last year and had received multiple messages beforehand stating I was eligible this year. When I calmly asked why I was suddenly not entitled, she repeatedly said she was not allowed to give me the vaccination, rather than addressing the actual question I was asking. She also told me that what I “really need to get done” was my smear test, stating it was long overdue. I informed her that I had this done two years ago and have the letter to prove it. She then checked and admitted it was recorded, suggesting the nurse must not have entered the correct code previously. Overall, the situation was handled poorly. While I understand issues like power cuts happen, the communication and attitude I experienced were disappointing. I left feeling frustrated and unheard. Due to repeated negative experiences, I am now in the process of changing GP practices. It is extremely disappointing to feel met with rudeness and attitude when attending appointments about my health. Patients should not feel uncomfortable or dismissed when seeking medical care. Professionalism and empathy are essential in healthcare settings. Unfortunately, my recent experiences have fallen far below what I would expect from a GP practice. No one should feel spoken to abruptly or made to feel like an inconvenience when they are simply trying to manage their health.
