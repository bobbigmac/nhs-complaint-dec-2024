Author: Eoghan Jones

Rating: 1 star
If you enjoy being demeaned or condescended to, this is the GP surgery for you! If you desire more human exchanges with individuals that contain a shred of empathy I would recommend you look elsewhere. The word negligence comes very vividly to mind when asked to describe my experience with this absolute mess of a surgery. The first instance of absolute lack of care came about when my non-verbal, autistic two year old son was fighting for breath one night. We subsequently contacted emergency services who were able to partly resolve the situation and were told to book in with the GP the next day to get them to follow up and make sure he was ok. The paramedics explained that, given the urgency, we should be seen pretty quickly. Following a chat with the receptionist the next morning, after my wife explained the urgency of my son’s situation, she was told “we have no appointments, call back tomorrow.” After reiterating that he was in a bad way and paramedics told us to be seen as soon as possible, my wife was put on hold while the receptionist spoke to her senior employee. Upon returning to the phone, she told my wife that unless my son was “mottled and white” we will have to call back tomorrow when an appointment may be available. Now, normally, if it were a fully talking, rational child, teen or adult that needed to be seen, I’d understand the lack of urgency, but given my son is highly vulnerable and unable to communicate, the response was appalling and, frankly, unacceptable. Now, as a fairly rational person, I like to think my levels of understanding are quite exceptional and my lack of initial reaction was due to the knowledge that the NHS can be stretched thin at times. However, this wasn’t the first and last instance of complete incompetence and lack of care shown. My wife has been out of town for the past few days (in Birmingham for a friend’s wedding). While away she suffered excruciating ear pain which generally indicates an incoming ear infection from which she regularly suffers from (and has been previously hospitalised with). Taking the initiative, and being away from home, yesterday she called the nearest GP surgery to where she was staying in Birmingham to see if she could get an emergency appointment. They happened to have an appointment available, which was a massive blessing as catching the ear infection early and treating it ASAP prevents unnecessary hospital visits and pain. The GP at this practice in Birmingham, following a consultation, prescribed medications to help with both the pain and infection. As my wife was due to return home, this prescription was sent to our local pharmacy for me, her husband, to collect on her behalf for when she arrived home, allowing her to take the meds straight away upon her return. When I arrived to collect them, I was told the pharmacist needed to make an enquiry and that the pharmacy would contact my wife when the meds were ready. Just as she arrived home, a female employee from St Richards Road Surgery telephoned my wife and asked about the nature of this prescription. When my wife had explained the situation, the person on the phone proceeded to scold my wife telling her that she should have completed an e-consult with her local surgery instead of seeking the immediate attention she required due to the nature of her condition. Now, if she weren’t in a different county, I would completely understand this logic, but being a few hundred miles away and around 3 and a half hours drive I struggle to see how my wife’s idea to seek nearby help is unreasonable in any way. Not only was this a completely illogical and ridiculous response, the woman who was addressing my wife on the phone was incredibly insensitive, condescending and generally rude about the whole situation. My wife was told that she couldn’t have her prescription, completely undermining the opinion of the other medical professional involved and denying my wife much needed treatment for her now worsening ear infection. Not only was my wife in agony, she was now anxiously crying. Great service.
