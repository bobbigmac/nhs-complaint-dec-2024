I recently had to retake a blood test at PHGH GP due to an abnormal result. During my visit, I was seen by an older nurse of Indian descent with short hair and a slender build. I want to share my experience to raise awareness and encourage better hygiene practices. Before the procedure, I informed the nurse that my previous blood test had been taken from my right arm just two days earlier. She assured me it was fine to use the same arm again. However, I noticed several concerning issues during the process: - Lack of hygiene: The nurse did not use an alcohol wipe to clean the area before inserting the needle. She also touched my skin with bare fingers while searching for a vein. - Multiple attempts: She attempted to draw blood twice from my right arm without success. I tried to help by pumping my fist, as is commonly requested elsewhere, but she asked me to stop. - Second attempt on the left arm: She then switched to my left arm and was able to draw blood, but again, the area was not cleansed beforehand. - No gloves or sanitization: At no point did I see gloves being used or proper sanitization steps being followed. These practices raise serious concerns about infection control and patient safety. The NHS should ensure that all staff are consistently trained in basic hygiene protocols, especially when performing procedures that involve skin puncture. The risk of transmitting infections, including serious ones, should not be underestimated. I’ve attached photos of my arms post-procedure as evidence. I urge others visiting this practice to kindly request that proper hygiene steps—such as wearing gloves and using alcohol wipes—are followed before any blood is drawn. Patient safety must always come first.
