I have been a patient at Argyle Medical Group in Pembroke Dock for several years, and the quality of service has declined sharply. A once‑reputable practice has become outdated and inefficient in meeting patient needs. Appointments are increasingly difficult to secure, with waits stretching days or longer. The practice appears unable to recruit and retain enough doctors, leaving patients with limited access to care. Phone lines remain the only contact method, yet they are constantly jammed. Even when I get through, hold times are excessive and appointments are already filled—a cycle of wasted time and frustration. Unlike other local surgeries, Argyle lags behind in adopting modern systems. Many practices now offer email consultations and video calls, but Argyle has resisted these improvements. Signs at the surgery even reveal that around 500 appointments go unused and uncancelled every month, a staggering waste that denies care to those who need it. Simple solutions exist: a reservation list for cancellations, releasing last‑minute slots online, or a modest £10 fine for missed appointments to fund a reliable booking system. More importantly, Argyle must embrace digital healthcare. Far more patients could be managed through email or secure video calls than in person, freeing phone lines and easing staff schedules. Platforms like WhatsApp are encrypted and proven, or Argyle could build its own secure system. Tenby already offers email consultations for minor issues, allowing patients to send photos and receive timely responses—reducing strain, freeing appointments, and keeping phone lines open. It is baffling that Argyle remains so resistant to modernisation. The solutions are obvious, effective, and already in use elsewhere. Until Argyle acts, patients and staff will continue to struggle under an outdated system that wastes resources and undermines care.
