I am a Type 1 diabetic and rely on uninterrupted access to insulin and glucose monitoring equipment to stay safe. My experience with this practice has been deeply concerning due to repeated administrative failures affecting essential diabetes care. Most recently, my repeat prescription for Humulin insulin — requested through the NHS App two days in advance — was not approved. When I called the surgery, I was told it had been referred for review and was advised to call back the next day. I had to explain that I was completely out of insulin and that my next injection was due that evening before the prescription was finally issued. For a Type 1 diabetic, insulin is life-sustaining medication. Delays or barriers to accessing it are not minor administrative issues — they carry immediate clinical risk. Being told to “call back tomorrow” when a patient has no insulin left demonstrates a worrying lack of urgency around continuity of care for chronic conditions. Unfortunately, this is not an isolated incident. Since registering with this practice I have experienced multiple problems affecting essential diabetes supplies, including a CGM prescription request not being processed (leaving me without monitoring for several days), initial refusal of basic glucose testing equipment, and repeated challenges around routine diabetes medication. Patients who depend on timely access to essential medication should be aware that delays and barriers can occur here. I sincerely hope the practice reviews its governance and prescribing processes so that other patients are not placed in similar risk.
