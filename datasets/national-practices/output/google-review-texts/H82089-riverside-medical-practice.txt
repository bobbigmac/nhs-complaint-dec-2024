Practice response date: a month ago
Practice response:
Dear Patient, We are writing further to your recent Google review and to clarify the position regarding your request for a private referral. We are sorry you felt disappointed with the care provided. We have stated clearly on our website that the practice is currently working extremely hard to meet demand for our NHS patients, particularly in the context of recent staff sickness and workforce pressures that are affecting many parts of the NHS. We operate a triage system to manage this demand safely and effectively, allowing us to respond quickly where appropriate and to direct patients to the most suitable NHS services when their needs fall outside the remit of general practice. This approach is designed to ensure that patients who unknowingly contact general practice for conditions that require other NHS services, such as Accident and Emergency or specialist assessment, are guided promptly and appropriately. Acute traumatic injuries fall into this category and are not conditions that general practice is commissioned, resourced, or funded to manage directly. We do offer some private, non-NHS services as a discretionary courtesy to patients, recognising that this can sometimes support their care, particularly in the context of long secondary care waiting times. These services sit outside NHS provision and are therefore chargeable. On this occasion, however, a private referral letter was provided free of charge within two days as a goodwill gesture, despite this not being resourced by the NHS and despite private work usually needing to be undertaken outside of NHS clinical commitments. We acted in this way to assist you promptly in an acute situation and to be supportive where possible. We were therefore disappointed that this effort, which was above and beyond our contracted NHS role, was not recognised in your subsequent public review. Given the evident mismatch between expectations and the role of general practice in providing private, non-NHS services, the practice will not be able to undertake any further private work on your behalf in the future. This decision does not affect your entitlement to NHS care, which will continue to be provided in line with NHS guidance and clinical need. Yours sincerely Dr Matthew Davies

Practice response date: 4 months ago
Practice response:
Thank you for your feedback. Like many GP practices across the country, we have moved to a total triage model. This is a shift from first-come, first-served telephone booking to clinical prioritisation based on the problem described. We have not made this change lightly. NHS staffing and funding constraints mean there are limits to the number of clinicians available, while demand for appointments continues to rise. Triage helps us match the available appointments to those who most need to be seen, including people with severe infections, suspected cancers, and other urgent issues. eConsult is part of this approach. It helps us identify problems that can safely be managed with self-care or community pharmacy and reserve face-to-face or telephone appointments for higher-risk concerns. We recognise the online questionnaire takes time to complete. The benefit is fewer unnecessary appointments and faster access for patients who need clinical assessment. For anyone who cannot use online forms, our reception team can help complete them or arrange alternatives. You have already told us about your experience with this process. While we cannot discuss individual details in a public forum, which limits our explanation here, we have responded about the advice that you were given by the doctor about your issue. We are sorry you feel let down. We respect your right to rate us, but a one-star score driven by one aspect of access does not reflect the care most patients receive or your wider experience with the practice. It risks doing a disservice to the team working within national guidance and severe resource limits. We cannot remove triage, but we will keep improving how we run it. If your overall care has been better than this one episode, please consider whether one star is a fair reflection.

Thank you for taking the time to share your concerns. We appreciate how worrying it is when a young child is unwell, particularly with a history of urine infections, and we take your feedback seriously. Whilst we value transparency and recognise that some families choose to post reviews on public forums such as Google, concerns about specific care are best communicated directly to the practice team. This is not because we do not wish to be open, but because we cannot explain your child’s individual care or clinical circumstances on a public platform due to confidentiality. Public comments can therefore present an unopposed and unbalanced view. Thankfully, we have been able to identify you from your feedback and will be writing to you directly with more detail about your son’s care. In broad terms, many children present with short-lived viral illnesses, and most recover without further intervention. However, we remain alert to the risk of more serious illness in children, including sepsis, and our systems are designed to identify this. You mention being seen by “only a nurse,” but it appears your son was reviewed by an Advanced Nurse Practitioner (ANP). These practitioners are highly qualified, competent clinicians, supervised by senior doctors. Without their support we would only be able to see a fraction of patients, which would be unsafe. The number of doctors available to practices is limited by national workforce constraints, so the use of ANPs ensures we can provide timely care for as many patients as possible. Being seen by an ANP does not mean the consultation was inadequate. Regarding urine testing, dipstick analysis can sometimes provide useful clues but is not highly specific and is prone to sampling error. Sending samples for formal laboratory analysis is often more appropriate, especially when suspicion of infection is low, as this provides an accurate bacterial diagnosis to guide treatment. We will be in touch with a personal response about your son’s case. In the meantime, we want to reassure you that children of this age, particularly those unable to communicate symptoms and with a relevant history, should and do receive prioritised care within our systems.
