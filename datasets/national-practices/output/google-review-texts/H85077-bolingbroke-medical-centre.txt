Author: Merve

Today, my husband and I visited this GP practice to request GP registration letters, which we’ve previously used as proof of address in our visa applications. Since we moved to this neighbourhood after our last application, we needed updated letters. The waiting area was nearly empty, just two other people, so the environment didn’t look busy at all. When we approached the reception desk, an older woman greeted us. We explained we were there to request our GP registration letters. She immediately made a confused facial expression, as if our request was unusual, and asked what the letter was for. Although we shouldn’t have needed to explain, we told her it was for our visa application to prove our place of residence, hoping to help clarify. She then confidently told us that we “wouldn’t need such a letter” for a visa application, speaking as though she were an expert on immigration requirements. She asked for our dates of birth, and shortly after claimed they couldn’t provide the letter because they were too busy. When I pointed out that it wasn’t a matter of policy but of being short on time, she couldn’t really respond. She left to check with someone else and came back saying that the letter could be provided but we needed to pay for it though she didn’t know how long it would take. She told us the GP would contact us when the letters were ready. Then we left. To begin with, we should have received our registration letters automatically when we first registered after moving to the area (years ago), there was no clear reason why we had to request them ourselves. What made the situation more difficult was the receptionist’s dismissive and unprofessional attitude. That kind of response, especially in a healthcare setting where people are already dealing with stress or uncertainty, is deeply disheartening. On top of that, the information we were given was confusing and contradictory. We were told the letter couldn’t be provided, then that it could be but we need to pay for it yet no one could say how much it would cost or how long it could take. We left without any clarity, just more uncertainty, all due to a brief but poorly handled exchange at reception. I usually try to avoid the reception of this GP altogether, managing my appointments through the app and speaking directly to the doctors. But what is this level of service, really? It’s very disappointing.
Practice response date: 7 months ago
Practice response:
Dear Merve, Thank you for taking the time to share your experience, and I’m truly sorry to hear that your recent visit to our practice left you feeling frustrated and disappointed. I’d like to clarify a few points regarding your request for a GP registration letter. The formal confirmation of GP registration is not issued by the GP practice but is instead sent directly by Primary Care Support England (PCSE) when a patient successfully registers with a GP. This letter serves as the official proof of registration and should be retained for future use. Requests for non-NHS administrative letters, such as proof of address for visa or immigration purposes, fall outside the scope of NHS-funded services and are therefore subject to an administrative fee. This charge helps cover the time and resources required to produce such documents. I agree this should have been explained to you more clearly, and I apologise for the confusion and inconsistency during your visit. For your reference, we have had detailed information about non-NHS work and associated charges available on our website for some time. Additionally, all administrative requests, including letters, forms, and similar documents, can be submitted online via our website, which helps streamline the process for both patients and staff. We are also sorry to hear that your interaction at reception was not handled in a more professional and courteous manner. We take such feedback seriously and I will be discussing it with the team to ensure a more consistent and respectful approach moving forward. If you still require the registration letter, please feel free to submit the request through our website, we will make sure it’s processed in accordance with our non-NHS work policy. Thank you again for bringing this to our attention. Kind regards, Practice Manager
