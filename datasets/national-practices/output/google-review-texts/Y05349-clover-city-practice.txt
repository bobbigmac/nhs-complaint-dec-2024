It's incredibly disappointing when a doctor lacks attentive listening skills. During my GP appointment, the doctor kept repeatedly asking, "How can I help you?" even after I had already begun explaining my concerns. This pattern continued throughout the consultation, making it clear that she wasn’t truly listening but was instead rushing to a conclusion. I even pointed out that I felt rushed, yet she insisted she wasn’t rushing me—only to immediately ask again, "How can I help you?" This constant interruption made me hesitant to share the full details of my condition. A patient needs to be heard, not repeatedly cut off with the same question. On top of that, she seemed unwilling to provide a detailed explanation of my illness, my diet, or other important aspects of my health, instead pushing brochures on me rather than taking the time to explain things properly. I had to keep pressing for answers before she finally provided more details. She also dismissed my symptoms—despite my persistent dizziness and fatigue—simply because nothing showed up in my blood tests. But symptoms matter, even if they aren’t reflected in lab results. When I tried to ask more about my blood test results—especially considering it had been three months since they were taken—she seemed uninterested, as if she didn’t understand the importance of the follow-up. Additionally, she never told me how much time was actually allocated for my appointment but kept reminding me that other patients were waiting. Every patient deserves their fair share of time and attention, yet I waited 25 minutes past my scheduled time only to receive a rushed 15-minute consultation. To make matters worse, when she checked my blood pressure, she simply said it "wasn't that low," without giving me the actual reading. Does a patient have to wait until it's dangerously low before it's considered a concern? This experience was extremely frustrating—poor communication, lack of empathy, and a dismissive attitude toward my condition. The level of service was unacceptable, especially considering the amount of money paid into the NHS.
