Author: Sarra

Rating: 1 star
Extremely Disappointed – Ongoing Issues With No Improvement This is my second review of this surgery, as no action appears to have been taken following my previous feedback. On Tuesday 24th February, I submitted an appointment request regarding ongoing neck and shoulder pain. The website states that the surgery aims to respond within two working days, which meant I expected contact by Thursday. On Thursday, I called and was told there were no available appointments. I called again on Friday and was told the same, but was reassured I would receive a phone call on Monday morning. That call never came. On Tuesday, I called again. In my original appointment request, I clearly stated that: • I am available for face-to-face appointments on Mondays and Tuesdays. • I can attend a telephone appointment Wednesday to Sunday, but it must be before 10am, as I start work after 10 and have no signal at my workplace. I was offered an appointment for Wednesday at 10:10am. I accepted, explaining that 10 minutes past 10 would still be manageable. I asked three times for confirmation that this was a telephone appointment, as I would be at work and unable to attend in person. I was reassured each time that it was a telephone appointment. On Wednesday, no one called. By 10:25am and then 10:40am, I still hadn’t received a call. When I phoned the surgery, I was told the appointment had been booked as face-to-face, not telephone. This was extremely frustrating, as I had clearly confirmed this multiple times. After explaining the situation, I was told the doctor would call within 20 minutes. I ended up waiting one hour and twenty minutes. During that time, I called twice more and was told each time that the GP would call in “10 minutes” which was not accurate. I was at work, without signal, and unable to wait by a window or doorway all day. I have responsibilities and cannot simply pause my job indefinitely. When the doctor finally called, we discussed my issue and he said he would need to see me in person. I explained the entire situation and asked if he could arrange the appointment himself, as I was having difficulty with reception. He said he would leave a note for reception and that I should call to confirm, and they would arrange it. When I called, the receptionist (Thillai) saw both my request and the doctor’s note but told me that booking was “not possible” and that I needed to submit another online request. This directly contradicted what the doctor had told me. At this point, I genuinely do not know where the breakdown is whether between doctors and reception, or due to unclear internal procedures. However, patients should not be caught in the middle of conflicting information. After asking to speak to a manager, I was told she was busy but would call me back. When she did, she managed to book me an after-hours appointment within two minutes. This proves that the issue was not availability it was process and communication. It should not require frustration, repeated phone calls, or escalation to management to receive basic assistance. This is a healthcare setting. People contacting the surgery are often in pain, worried, or vulnerable. Not everyone is confident using online systems, particularly elderly patients. Simply directing everyone to “do it online” is not patient-centred care. The overall experience has been unprofessional, poorly communicated, and deeply disappointing. I sincerely hope the management reviews internal processes, as this level of service is unacceptable.
