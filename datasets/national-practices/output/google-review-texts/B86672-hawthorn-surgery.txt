Author: Chris
Date: 5 months ago
Rating: 1 star
After waiting for appointments that are urgently required, patients are often sent away without proper help or support. The doctors frequently dismiss serious concerns, and you’re only allowed to discuss one issue per appointment, even if your problems are clearly related. I was left without proper care despite attending my appointment, and the staff refused to treat me due to lateness (due to access issues, I was 10 mins early on site). It is important to note that NHS-funded practices receive payment for each booked appointment regardless of whether the patient is seen or the issue is addressed. This creates a situation where practices are financially compensated even when patients do not receive the care they urgently need, which is a systemic flaw and not a one-off incident. Access is also a major problem: the car park is overcrowded, making it almost impossible to arrive on time, and there’s little understanding or flexibility when this happens. Overall, the service is disorganised, uncaring, and leaves patients feeling abandoned. This is a diabolical level of care, a surgery to be avoided at all costs. I would like to stress the urgency of my condition. An infected wound, particularly one showing signs of spreading or possible sepsis, can rapidly become life-threatening if left untreated. Despite the clear medical urgency and the unavoidable lateness of my arrival, the surgery showed little to no concern for my health or the potential severity of the infection. This lack of compassion and medical responsibility is deeply concerning and unacceptable in an NHS-funded practice.
