Practice response date: 5 months ago
Practice response:
Hello Anthony, Thank you for sharing your experience — I’m sorry it’s been frustrating. Demand is very high and we know it can feel like a runaround when online slots show as unavailable. Here’s how to get help and why the system is set up this way: Our lines open at 8am for urgent and routine requests. If it’s clinically urgent, please say so to the care navigator so it can be prioritised. You can come to reception during opening hours. If you struggle with forms/online access, our team can help submit a request on your behalf. Submit a non‑urgent request during core hours (8:00–18:30). When safe capacity is reached for the day, further routine requests are added to a waiting list or advised to try the next day, in line with BMA safe working guidance. Pharmacists can manage a wide range of minor illnesses and, under Pharmacy First, some conditions can be treated without a GP appointment. Evening and weekend GP appointments are available locally via our PCN/federation. Reception can book you into these if appropriate. We explain all access routes, including urgent vs routine pathways and how we prioritise care. I am happy to explain why we can’t return to walk‑in/open surgery: Open surgery creates an “8am queue” at the practice and can lead to unsafe, untriaged queues with long waits. Clinical triage and care navigation let us match patients to the right clinician and timeframe, focusing same‑day capacity on those with the greatest clinical need. GP practices receive limited funding and have to balance high demand with safe staffing. Capping sessions and spreading capacity prevents burnout and helps maintain continuity and quality of care. This approach aligns with BMA safe working guidance and current NHS England access requirements.
