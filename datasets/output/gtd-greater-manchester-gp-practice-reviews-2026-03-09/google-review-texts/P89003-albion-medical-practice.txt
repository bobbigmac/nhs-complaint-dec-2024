Author: Michael Keating
Date: 3 months ago
Rating: 1 star
I felt that my health problems were dismissed and was disappointed and frustrated with the consultation overall. I had registered with the surgery for the GP to consider shared care protocol for ADHD medication prescribed and monitored by a private company I have paid for monthly since January 2025. I'd been advised by the receptionist prior to registering that this would at the least be considered. During my first consultation I was told indefinitely that the practice had a 'blanket rule' against prescribing in this manner. I would have accepted that, as I did with my previous practice, however I feel I was then delivered misinformation that was disheartening and upsetting. The GP told me that 'these private companies will give anybody a diagnosis of ADHD', something which I feel particularly upset and frustrated by. I know for a fact, as a specialist nurse for psychiatry working in primary care that there are many people referred for an ADHD assessment that are not diagnosed but offered alternatives and private companies will make recommendations based on valuable assessments that patients benefit from regardless. For a medical professional to deliver this false information can be especially damaging to somebody in my position, having waited years and paid excessively for support with. I, like many other service users have found relief and in the very least been validated by a diagnosis of ADHD which has answered a lot of questions and provided a pathway to improved wellbeing and hope for the future. The GP also stated that these private companies do not offer a true 'shared care' approach, and that if there are any problems with prescribing in primary care there is no support available from them. I know that this is also false as I have been instructed to remain under the care of the private company and pay for six monthly reviews from the specialists working alongside primary services to ensure safe prescribing and management of medications. The GP also attempted to misinform me that private companies prescribe medications without ensuring safe titration and effective physical health monitoring. Again, I know for a fact that I was sufficiently monitored in terms of bloods, ECG and regular blood pressure and weight reviews since being diagnosed with ADHD in January 2025. In summary, I feel Albion Medical Group have been negligent and failed to deliver individualised care based on service user need. Not only that, but many of the false statements assumed by the GP could have been particularly damaging to vulnerable people with a diagnosis of ADHD, further marginalising people like myself trying to seek support for this disability. If the surgery have a 'blanket rule' against considering shared care protocols for ADHD medication, they should at least ensure the GP's are better informed and work harder not to further dismiss and discredit patients already struggling to access safe support and guidance.
