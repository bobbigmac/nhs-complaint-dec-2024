# ESCALATION.md

## Scope / aim

This document is a practical escalation ladder for **digital exclusion + access barriers** at **New Bank Health Centre (GoToDoc / “GTD”)** — specifically:
- “Digital-only” or “PATCHS-only” access pathways that block patients who can’t use PATCHS reliably.
- **Mandatory unscheduled phone calls** (missed-call loops, “wait all day” requirement) used as a gate to care.
- PATCHS downtime/capacity shutoffs used as an access throttle without an effective non-digital fallback.
- Staff messaging that implies there is no alternative route (“you have to do it online”).

Primary goal: get the practice/commissioner to **change the operating model** so people can access care safely and fairly.
Secondary goal: create a paper trail so the issue is visible to regulators and oversight bodies if it isn’t fixed.

---

## Hard policy hooks

### 1) Core-hours online access requirement (Oct 2025 onward)
NHS England requirement: from **1 Oct 2025**, practices must keep their **online consultation tool open throughout core hours** (8:00–18:30) at least for:
- non-urgent appointment requests
- medication queries
- admin requests

NHS England also states practices have been required to ensure patients can contact them by **phone or walking in throughout core hours** (since 2004), and the aim is patients can contact their practice by **phone, online, or walking in**.

Reference:
- https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/online-consultations-frequently-asked-questions/

### 2) Equity / equal access expectation
CQC’s “equity in access” expectation is relevant where access models disadvantage groups (disability, language, digital literacy, poverty, shift work, etc.).
Reference:
- https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/assessment/single-assessment-framework/responsive/equity-access

### 3) Accessible Information Standard (AIS)
If communication needs or impairments are in play (including needing scheduled calls instead of unscheduled, or written comms), use AIS as a formal hook.
Reference:
- https://www.england.nhs.uk/accessible-information-standard/

## What “good” looks like (define the minimum fixes)

Ask for specific operational commitments, for example:
1) A **non-digital route** that is *real* (not “submit online so we can decide to call you”):
   - walk-in / reception request capture OR
   - phone request capture (with call-back windows) OR
   - assisted-digital support at reception
2) No mandatory unscheduled calls as the only route:
   - allow **scheduled call windows** and/or **message-based** handling where appropriate
3) PATCHS reliability safeguards:
   - clear outage fallback route (phone/walk-in) communicated to patients
4) Staff script change:
   - staff must not say “digital only”; they must offer alternatives
5) Transparent access standards:
   - published hours, response expectations, and what happens when capacity is hit

## Escalation ladder (do in order, but you can parallel-run some steps)

### Step 1 — Practice (already started, but formalise the request)
**Target:** Practice Manager / complaints lead; copy the GP Partners (where possible).  
**Purpose:** give them a clean chance to fix it; lock in commitments.

Include:
- A concise summary + evidence pack link/attachments.
- A short list of “minimum fixes” (see above).
- A deadline for a written response and a follow-up date.

Also request (in writing):
- Their current **access policy** (digital/phone/walk-in) and any internal SOP.
- Their **downtime/capacity** policy for PATCHS (“when full”, “off during core hours”, etc.).
- Whether they consider themselves “digital only” (force them to deny it in writing).

If you have personal communication needs:
- Make a **reasonable adjustment / AIS** request (e.g., “no unscheduled calls; use scheduled window or written comms”).

---

### Step 2 — NHS Greater Manchester ICB (commissioner) — CONTRACT MANAGEMENT ESCALATION
**Target:** ICB Patient Services / Primary Care commissioning team.  
**Purpose:** this is the body that can apply contract-performance pressure and require an improvement plan.

Send:
- The 1-page `summary.md`
- `timeline.md`
- key screenshots
- the minimum fixes list

**Key questions to force commissioner ownership:**
1) “Has the practice been granted approval to switch off/close PATCHS during core hours or restrict access due to capacity? If yes, provide dates and rationale.”
2) “What contract performance standards apply for equitable access and non-digital routes for this APMS provider, and how are they monitored?”
3) “What immediate mitigations will be required while a longer-term redesign is implemented?”

**ICB contact points (as of last check):**
- Patient Services (complaints): nhsgm.patientservices@nhs.net
- FOI (for later steps): nhsgm.foi@nhs.net
- Postal: NHS Greater Manchester, Tootal, 56 Oxford St, Manchester M1 6EU

ICB complaints routing reference:
- https://www.england.nhs.uk/contact-us/feedback-and-complaints/complaint/

---

### Step 3 — ICB Governance visibility (Primary Care committee / locality leadership)
**Target:** ICB Primary Care governance route (committee / locality leads).  
**Purpose:** ensure this becomes a governance item, not “one unhappy patient”.

Actions:
- Ask (in writing) for the issue to be logged as:
  - “Primary care access equity / digital exclusion risk”
  - “contract performance concern”
- Request it be considered at the relevant primary care commissioning governance meeting.
- Request a named ICB owner and written action plan.

---

### Step 4 — CQC feedback (regulatory risk signal; not casework)
**Target:** CQC “Give feedback on care”.  
**Purpose:** feed the regulator’s risk model (equity/access barrier).

Make the feedback about:
- access barriers
- digital exclusion
- lack of reasonable adjustments
- safety risk from delays / inability to contact

CQC feedback portal:
- https://www.cqc.org.uk/give-feedback-on-care

---

### Step 7 — Local democratic oversight (Manchester City Council Health Scrutiny)
**Target:** Manchester City Council Health Scrutiny Committee / scrutiny mailbox.  
**Purpose:** local oversight pressure; requests for the NHS to explain access models publicly.

Committee info + contact:
- https://democracy.manchester.gov.uk/mgCommitteeDetails.aspx?ID=142
- scrutiny@manchester.gov.uk (scrutiny landing page lists it)
- Committee support officer (listed on committee page; verify current): lee.walker@manchester.gov.uk

How to frame it:
- “A commissioned GP service has an operating model that excludes digitally excluded residents; seeking scrutiny attention on equitable access.”

---

### Step 8 — Patient safety incident reporting (when access failures create risk)
If delayed access created a safety incident or near miss (missed cancer red flags, medication risk, worsening condition due to inability to get through), report it.

NHS England states the **public can report** patient safety events to LFPSE:
- https://www.england.nhs.uk/patient-safety/patient-safety-insight/learning-from-patient-safety-events/report-patient-safety-incident/
- Anonymous entry point:
  https://record.learn-from-patient-safety-events.nhs.uk/

This is not “complaints”; it’s “safety learning” and can get attention when framed cleanly.

---

### Step 9 — MP / Councillors (political pressure)
**Target:** your MP + local councillors (especially where health inequalities or digital exclusion portfolios exist).  
**Purpose:** increase heat on the commissioner/provider; request written explanations and meetings.

Send them:
- 1-page summary
- specific ask:
  - “Please write to NHS GM ICB asking what contract levers are being used to address digital exclusion and access barriers at New Bank Health Centre.”

---

### Step 10 — FOI (to stop the story being hand-waved)
**Target:** NHS Greater Manchester FOI.  
**Purpose:** obtain documents that reveal expectations vs reality.

FOI email:
- nhsgm.foi@nhs.net
FOI page:
- https://gmintegratedcare.org.uk/freedom-of-information/

High-yield FOI requests (keep narrow to avoid refusal):
1) Any “access model” requirements in the APMS contract for New Bank Health Centre (or the relevant service spec sections).
2) Any commissioner approvals / correspondence relating to switching off online consultation tools during core hours.
3) Performance/KPI reports or monitoring summaries for access, digital inclusion, complaints, and patient experience for the service since contract start.
4) Equality impact assessments (if held) relating to the access model.

---

### Step 11 — Ombudsman (endgame after final response)
**Target:** Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman (PHSO).  
**Purpose:** independent review when local resolution fails.

Precondition:
- You normally need a **final response** from the organisation you complained to (practice/ICB).

PHSO:
- https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/

## Parallel “interest groups” that can add pressure (optional, situation-dependent)

These are not formal complaint routes but can help build pressure, evidence, or coalition:
- Digital inclusion / poverty / disability charities (local + national)
- Age UK (digital exclusion in older adults)
- RNIB / Action on Hearing Loss-style groups (communication barriers)
- Citizens Advice (access + admin friction + rights signposting)
- Community groups in Longsight/Belle Vue (language and access barriers)

Approach: share the 1-page summary and invite anonymised co-signing.

---

## How to keep it professional (and still unmistakable)

Use a consistent closing paragraph:

> “I’m asking for the access model to be fixed locally and with a clear and open timeline. If it isn’t, I will escalate through the commissioner, regulator, Healthwatch, local scrutiny, and (where relevant) patient safety reporting, using the evidence attached.”

Avoid:
- motives, insults, speculation about individuals
- “they don’t care” style language

Do:
- dates, screenshots, quotes, outcomes, and the minimum fixes list
