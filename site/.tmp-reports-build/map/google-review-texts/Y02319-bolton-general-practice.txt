Author: Royalty x Tehmeh
Date: Edited 16 hours ago
Rating: 1 star
I’m writing this as a patient who feels deeply let down by the GP system in Bolton. I developed what started as a cold burn, which has since turned into an open wound. It has still not healed and remains untreated properly despite me contacting my GP surgery on two separate occasions. During my first visit, I was prescribed antibiotics. What concerned me most was that the doctor appeared unsure about the condition and searched online during the appointment to understand what it was and how to treat it. Seeing a medical professional rely on an internet search in front of me made me feel unsafe and worried that they didn’t fully understand what they were dealing with. I was given antibiotics and sent home, but there was no proper wound care, cleaning or dressing provided. The wound has still not healed. I attended again on the 11 march because I was worried about infection and the lack of improvement. I waited for around an hour past my appointment time due to other issues at the practice, which I understand can happen when services are extremely busy. However, when I was finally seen, the appointment itself lasted only about two- three minutes. The doctor briefly looked at the wound and said I would be referred elsewhere. When I asked how long that would take, I was told “not long — around two weeks.” I left feeling shocked and frustrated and I’m not someone who is outspoken so I couldn’t even ask the questions I truly wanted to ask, especially when I feel rushed in an environment. I don’t understand why a GP service cannot provide basic wound care such as cleaning the injury and applying the correct dressing. Being told to wait weeks with an open wound feels unacceptable and distressing. I understand GP practices are under pressure and very busy and I am willing to put some delays down to how packed the surgery was. However, patients should still feel properly assessed, treated, and reassured especially when dealing with an open wound that risks 🥀infection🥀. This situation has left me asking some serious questions about our health care quality • What is the role of a GP if basic treatment isn’t provided? • Why are patients left waiting with untreated wounds? • What are we paying taxes for if essential frontline care isn’t accessible? My visit to this GP has shown me that quantity over quality is more valued. I understand the pressures on the National Health Service and that staff are often overstretched. However, patients should still feel safe, listened to and properly treated. Right now, I feel dismissed and let down by a system that is supposed to provide timely and 🥀competent care🥀.
