# A clearer picture of GP access failure, pressure and recovery

This site is the public-facing summary of a larger evidence project about **patient access problems, experience signals and recovery needs** around **New Bank Health Centre**, **GTD Healthcare**, and the wider Greater Manchester practice landscape.

It is deliberately evidence-led and constructive. The aim is not to turn poor performance into theatre. The aim is to make the pattern visible enough that the right people can **fix the workflow, reduce exclusion, improve the front door, raise the scores and rebuild trust**.

## What this project is tracking

### 1. Access routes that can exclude patients

The repo documents repeated concerns about **digital-first access**, rigid contact routes, office-hours gating and process designs that are easier for systems than for patients. For some people the problem is not a single bad interaction. It is a repeated pattern of friction, delay and restart.

### 2. Request closure loops and continuity loss

One of the most serious practical problems raised in this project is the way a missed call, a failed handoff or a hard-to-complete triage step can dump the patient back at the start. That does not just feel frustrating. It can compound delay, reduce continuity and make unresolved health issues harder to carry forward.

### 3. Reception pressure and front-door behaviour

The repo includes complaints, review patterns and meeting notes pointing to a front desk environment where patients can experience the service as rigid, dismissive or harder to navigate when they are already stressed. That is partly a staffing issue, partly a workflow issue, and partly a management issue.

### 4. Management, oversight and takeover context

This is not only a New Bank story. The wider dataset looks at GTD-managed practices in context, including **documented takeover dates**, local comparators and how experience has moved over time. That matters because a new operator does not inherit a blank slate, but it does inherit responsibility for the recovery.

### 5. Wider questions beyond GTD and Manchester

The project also surfaced a bigger structural issue in the published data: many NHS branch surgery codes do not appear as standalone GP Patient Survey practices. That can make branch-level experience harder to interpret unless the parent-practice relationship is handled carefully. The latest map now resolves those branch codes to the NHS-published parent survey records so that the comparison is more honest and complete.

## Why the map matters

The interactive map is the fastest way to move from anecdote to pattern. It brings together:

- GTD-managed practices and nearby comparators across Greater Manchester
- Google scores, review coverage and review text capture where available
- GP Patient Survey experience and completion metrics
- GTD takeover dates on the portfolio trend chart
- Registered patient counts and management-company grouping where identifiable

That combination matters because no single metric can explain what is happening. Reviews, survey scores, management changes and access complaints all show different parts of the same problem.

## Shared purpose

This project works best if it is read with a **shared-purpose lens**:

- identify where people are being blocked, lost or worn down
- separate inherited problems from current management choices
- make it easier to measure recovery, not just decline
- improve access in ways that are fair to patients and sustainable for staff
- use the local case to ask harder questions about broader NHS access and survey blind spots

## What comes next

This landing page is intentionally lean. More material will be added once the structure for explaining the problems is clearer. The immediate priority is to keep the evidence bundle easy to navigate, easy to download and easy to update without introducing a lot of site machinery.
